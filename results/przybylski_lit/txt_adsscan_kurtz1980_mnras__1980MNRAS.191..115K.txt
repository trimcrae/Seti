                        1 Introduction

                        HD 101065, Przybylski’s star, has a magnetic field of 5 = — 2200 G (Wolff & Hagen
                        1976), an extremely complex spectrum rich with lines of the rare earths and lanthanides,
                        and is a light variable with a period of 12.14min (Kurtz 1978; Kurtz & Wegner 1979 -
                        Paper I). Very little else which is known about this star has not been vigorously debated in
                        the literature. In Paper I, it was argued that the known observations of HD 101065 could be
                        fitted by an extreme Ap star model with reff=7400K and a luminosity near the main
                        sequence. Paper I gives both the arguments leading to that model and references to the
                        earlier literature.
                            In this paper, we wish to focus attention on the interesting properties of the light
                        variability of HD 101065. In Section 2 we report new high-speed photometric observations
                        of HD 101065 which cover more than 230 cycles of the variability in both Johnson B and
                        V colours. In Section 3 a Fourier Transform frequency analysis of the light variability is
                        given with a least squares fit of the derived frequencies to the data. A discussion of the
                        frequency analysis is given in Section 4, where pulsation in the / = 2 non-radial mode is
                        identified.

                        3 Frequency analysis

                        Frequency analysis of several subsets of the data listed in Table 1 were carried out using a
                        discrete Fourier Transform for unequally spaced data (Deeming 1975). To completely
                        resolve two frequencies with this technique, they should be separated by at least 1.5/AT in
                        frequency space where AT is the time span of the data set (Loumos & Deeming 1978). The
                        central peak associated with a frequency in the power spectrum typically has a half-width
                        about one-third of this value. Where no cycle count ambiguities exist and where all deter-
                        mined frequencies are adequately resolved, this half-width should be a satisfactory repre-
                        sentation of the errors in the frequency determination.
                           The frequency analyses were carried out on the Johnson B data only since the amplitude
                        of the variations in V are much smaller than in B. The frequencies derived for various
                        subsets of the data are given in Table 2. The errors given are 1.5/Ar and, in parentheses,
                        one-third of that value. The frequencies were derived by successively pre-whitening the data
                        by the frequency with the highest peak in the power spectrum.

                            In Table 4 we have tabulated A(p(V,B-V) as predicted by the Balona & Stobie (1979)
                        theory for various values of / and f and assuming the phase lag is i// = 90° which is
                        appropriate for adiabatic pulsation. Non-adiabaticity effects do not greatly effect
                        A0(K, Æ—F) for 60° ^ 0 < 150°. The parameter /measures the relative contributions to the
                        light variations of the temperature and radius variations at a given point on the star. For
                        large values of/, the temperature variation dominates the light variation and the phase shifts
                        A0(F, Æ-F) become small. For all odd values of / the phase shift A0(F, Æ-F) = 0
                        independent of/.
                            The measured value of A(f)(V, B—V) = 39° ±3° for the principal frequency in HD 101065
                        is thus strongly indicative of pulsation in the / =2 quadrupole mode. Pulsation in odd /
                        modes (A<p(V, B-V) = 0) and pulsation in radial modes (A(p(V, B-V) < 0) are effectively
                        ruled out. Probably, so is pulsation in modes with / > 4. For reasonable values of /- 10
                        which are typically found for Cepheids and Ô Scuti stars, A0(V, B—V) predicted for / > 4 is
                        much larger than the observed phase shift for the principal frequency of 39° ±3°. For the
                        / = 2 mode, this phase shift is indicative of rather low values of / as can be seen in Table 4.
                        No prediction of the exact value off can be made, however, since we do not know 0 and
                        deviations from the adiabatic case of 0 = 90° will produce small changes in the predicted
                        A0(F, B-V) for a given value of /. As long as (i) smaller B-Vis associated with higher Teff,
                        (ii) the phase lag is in the approximate range 60° < 0 < 150° and (iii)/^ 20 in HD 101065,
                        then the conclusion that the principal frequency is due to pulsation in the 1 = 2 quadrupole
                        mode is reasonably secure. All of the above conditions should be met if the Ap star model
                        of HD 101065 which was proposed in Paper I is correct. In that case the spectral peculiarities
                        of HD 101065 are due to surface abundance anomalies and should not strongly affect the
                        pulsation driving mechanism (and hence 0 and /) in this star.
                            The amplitude of the B—V variations in HD 101065 are larger than the amplitude of the
                         V variations which is quite unusual. At first glance this might seem to indicate a relatively
                        large temperature variation factor and hence a large value of /. However, the extreme line
                        blanketing in the spectrum of HD 101065 makes it impossible to correlate the B-V variation
                        with a temperature variation as the amount of energy redistributed to unobserved wave-
                        lengths is unknown. Ultraviolet observations from IUE in 1980 planned by Dr P. B. Byrne
                        may help to resolve this problem.

                            Little can be said about the nature of the pulsation mode that gives rise to /3. The period
                        ratio P1/P3 = 0.958 is not informative since it only tells us that P! and P3 cannot both be
                        due to radial modes and we already know that Px is associated with a non-radial mode.
                        Similarly, the phase shift for /3 is not informative since the errors allow virtually any mode.
                            It was pointed out in Paper I that the very short principal period in HD 101065 of
                        Pi = 12.14min is difficult to explain. If one wishes to associate this period with a pulsation
                        mode having a low value of the radial quantum number, fc, then a low mass or sub-dwarf
                        model is required. This does not seem very likely as most other observations of HD 101065
                        are consistent with a Population I, extreme Ap star model with a luminosity near the main
                        sequence. If this is correct, then a high value of the radial quantum number, « 13 (Paper I)
                        may be necessary to explain Pi - 12.14min. Any model of HD 101065 which suggests a
                        particular fc-value for Pi must also predict the existence of a nearby radial or non-radial
                        mode which fits P3. Thus the existence of P3 may be a very useful constraint on theoretical
                        predictions of the value of fc.
