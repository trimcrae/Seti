ABSTRACT
We search for galaxy-scale (Dysonian) waste heat in the mid-infrared using WISE. Starting from the
2MASS Redshift Survey (2MRS), we cross-match to CatWISE2020 and AllWISE, apply standard MIR
AGN/starburst vetoes (Stern, Assef R90, Jarrett), and treat W1 and W2 as stellar baselines and W3
and W4 as constraining bands. For each galaxy and for blackbody waste heat temperatures T = 150–
600 K, we convert W3/W4 photometry into conservative 3σ per-galaxy upper limits on the bolometric
waste heat luminosity using the WISE bandpass (RSR) color correction. The resulting distributions
have median caps of ∼ (5–9) × 108 L⊙ across T = 150–600 K. Aggregated at the population level, the
one-sided 95% upper bound on the fraction of nearby galaxies that could host waste heat above a given
threshold monotonically decreases with threshold and asymptotes to ≃ 1/6500 at high thresholds (set
by the sample size). Sensitivity transitions from W4 at T ≲ 200 K to W3 at T ≳ 300 K. Interpreted
with the AGENT formalism, a fiducial Milky Way like stellar luminosity L⋆ = 3 × 1010 L⊙ implies
typical per galaxy caps of α ≡ Lwh /L⋆ ≲ 1.7–2.9% over T = 150–600 K (e.g., α ≲ 1.8% at T =300 K).
At T ≃ 300 K, no more than f95 ≃ 1.61 × 10−4 (≃ 0.0161%) of nearby galaxies can host KIII-scale
systems reprocessing ≳ 21% of a Milky Way–like stellar luminosity into ∼ 300 K waste heat.
1. INTRODUCTION

A basic consequence of energy use is the disposal of
low entropy energy as heat (F. J. Dyson 1960). If
galaxy scale engineering reprocesses a significant fraction of starlight or other power sources, the associated waste heat should emerge as approximately thermal emission with a characteristic temperature of a few
×102 K, peaking in the mid-infrared (MIR). This motivates “Dysonian SETI” (R. J. Bradbury et al. 2011;
M. M. Ćirković & R. J. Bradbury 2006), which seeks
astrophysical waste heat signatures rather than deliberate beacons. B. C. Lacki (2016) show that extremely
cold (≪ 100 K) galaxy scale “blackboxes” would violate
microwave constraints and are therefore strongly disfavored. All-sky FIR/MIR searches began with IRAS,
with early constraints on Dyson-like structures presented by R. A. Carrigan (2009). With WISE (E. L.
Wright et al. 2010) and NEOWISE (A. Mainzer et al.
2014), Ĝ program (J. T. Wright et al. 2014b,a) set quantitative WISE-based upper limits on Kardashev Type III
systems; subsequent work tightly constrained the reddest extended sources (R. L. Griffith et al. 2015).

Two developments now motivate a re–examination of
galaxy–scale waste–heat constraints. First, the CatWISE2020 catalog (F. Marocco et al. 2021), built on
WISE and the extended NEOWISE time baseline, delivers improved W1/W2 astrometry and photometry
across the entire sky, enabling cleaner baselines for modeling stellar continua in galaxies. Second, lessons from
the WISE /AllWISE (R. M. Cutri et al. 2013; R. M.
Cutri & AllWISE Team 2014) artifact taxonomy and the
maturation of MIR AGN/starburst diagnostics (e.g., D.
Stern et al. 2012; R. J. Assef et al. 2018; see also the
widely used color wedges of T. H. Jarrett et al. 2011)
allow us to construct transparent selection and masking policies that substantially reduce non–technological
false positives.
In this paper we leverage CatWISE2020 (for W1/W2)
together with AllWISE (for W1,W2,W3,W4) to place
population constraints on galaxy–scale waste heat for
a nearby, well–characterized parent sample.

Our analysis converts WISE photometry and flags into
per–galaxy waste–heat upper limits and then into population–level bounds. For transparency, each step is
specified by explicit equations and a short pseudo code
workflow (Appendix A). All configuration choices (artifact policy, AGN/starburst masks, temperature grid,
and significance level) are fixed before inspecting the
population results.

3.1. Band Choice and Artifact Policies
We treat W1/W2 as the stellar baseline and W3/W4
as the constraining bands for waste heat. Band selection
follows:
• W1/W2 (baseline): prefer CatWISE magnitudes when ab flags=0 and the per–band character of cc flags is “0” (clean). If CatWISE fails
these criteria, we fall back to AllWISE provided
its cc flags is clean in that band.
• W3/W4 (constraints): use AllWISE.
We define two artifact policies that differ only in the
treatment of W4; in all cases W1, W2, and W3 must be
clean in their respective bands:
STRICT: W1, W2, and W3 require cc flags character “0” (clean); W4 must also have cc flags “0”.
LENIENT: Identical to STRICT for W1, W2 and W3;
W4 is retained even if its cc flags character is
non-zero (retained with a flag).

3. Jarrett wedge: (W 2−W 3) ≥ 1.3, (W 1−W 2) ≤
1.7, and (W 1−W 2) ≥ 0.10 (W 2−W 3) + 0.38 and
≥ 0.315 (W 2−W 3) − 0.222.
Our analysis uses the union mask (Stern+Assef+Jarrett).
We also evaluate variants (none; Stern only;
Stern+Assef) as robustness checks (Fig. 3).
A KIII-like MIR excess moves sources toward large
W 2−W 3 and moderately large W 1−W 2, overlapping
the starburst/AGN locus. If such systems exist, they
would be removed by our union mask when we require an astrophysically clean retained sample. However, our population limits are set by the number of
galaxies for which our data would have been sensitive at a given threshold (Neff ). Figure 3 shows that
using no mask, Stern only, Stern+Assef, or the full
union yields statistically indistinguishable f95 curves:
the asymptotic plateaus and mid-threshold behavior are
unchanged within our precision.

4. RESULTS

4.1. Working Sample and Masks
Starting from the 2MRS parent list (§2),
cross–matching to CatWISE2020 and AllWISE with
the best–candidate protocol of §2.3 yields a working set
of ∼ 2.2 × 104 galaxies with usable MIR photometry.
The Strict sample requires clean per–band artifact
flags for all four bands, whereas the Lenient variant
retains W4 even if flagged (§3.1). We then apply the
union of three independent MIR AGN/starburst masks
(Stern, Assef R90, and a Jarrett–style wedge; §3.2). The
retained sources occupy the locus of normal galaxies in
WISE color–color space, while masked objects cluster
in the expected AGN/starburst regions (Fig. 6). After all selections, the number of galaxies with a valid
per–object limit is temperature–independent and equals
Nvalid = 19,617 for the Strict+full–mask sample (Table 1).

5.3. Non-detections and a Common kσ Convention
Catalog non–detections (ph qual=U) are mapped to
the same significance as detections via Eq. 3, with
αU ≡ k/kU and k = 3, kU ≃ 2. Varying kU over [1.5, 3]
modifies only the faint-end caps; the f95 curves at bright
thresholds and their convergence are insensitive because
f95 ∝ 1/Neff and Neff is dominated by objects with firm
detections or strong limits.

6. DISCUSSION
6.1. What the Population Bounds Mean
Our primary result is a set of temperature–dependent
population bounds, f95 (T, Lwh,thr ), that quantify what

fraction of nearby galaxies could host waste–heat luminosity above a threshold Lwh,thr while remaining consistent with the observed WISE W3/W4 photometry under
our kσ flux–cap model. Because f95 ∝ 1/Neff at bright
thresholds, the high–threshold plateaus reflect only the
effective sample size: with Nvalid = 19,617 (Table 1), all
five curves converge to f95 ≃ 3/Nvalid ≃ 1.53 × 10−4 .
At lower thresholds the curves separate in the physically expected order (Fig. 2), encapsulating the different
leverage of W3 and W4 on cool vs. warm blackbodies.
We intentionally refrain from imposing host–galaxy
priors when presenting limits. For context, however, the
single–object medians in Table 1 span p50 ≃ (5.0–8.6) ×
108 L⊙ from T = 150–600 K. If one adopts a representative stellar luminosity L⋆ ∼ few ×1010 L⊙ , these caps
correspond to order–percent fractional waste heat per
galaxy; the exact mapping is population–dependent and
not required for our conclusions.

6.4. Dependence on Radiator Temperature and a Path
to Improvement
The limiting–band statistics (Fig. 5) provide a practical roadmap. For cool radiators (T ≤ 200 K), W4
(22 µm) controls sensitivity; deeper W4 imaging, improved large–scale background modeling, or better artifact rejection would directly tighten the cool–T curves.

the greatest leverage. Multi–wavelength anchoring of
the long–wavelength SED (e.g., with far–IR photometry) would further reduce dependence on MIR color selection and sharpen the interpretation of red outliers.

7. CONCLUSIONS

We presented a WISE based search for Dysonian waste
heat in nearby galaxies. The analysis (i) implements
bandpass integrated relative spectral response (RSR)
color corrections for W3/W4, (ii) treats non detections
with a σ–consistent flux cap, and (iii) prefers profile–
fit photometry, with extended source photometry assessed as a systematic. Applied to a ∼ 2.2 × 104 –
object working sample, the Strict+full–mask subset
yields Nvalid = 19,617 per object limits across T ∈
{150, 200, 300, 400, 600} K.
Our main findings are:
1. The population bounds f95 (T, Lwh,thr ) are monotonic in threshold and converge at large Lwh,thr to
the binomial plateau f95 ≈ 3/Nvalid ≃ 1.53 × 10−4
(Figs. 2, 4).
2. The limiting band transitions from W4 at T ≤
200 K to W3 at T ≥ 300 K (Fig. 5), explaining the
families of curves and highlighting where deeper
data would most improve sensitivity.
3. Proper W3/W4 bandpass treatment matters at
mid thresholds: relative to a monochromatic mapping, the RSR correction raises individual caps by
∼16% at 300 K (and ∼9% at 400 K), while leaving
the high–threshold plateau fixed by Neff .
4. Mask choices, artifact policy (STRICT vs. LENIENT), and RJ subtraction induce only modest,
shape–preserving changes; the conclusions are robust (Fig. 3).

A. WORKFLOW PSEUDO-CODE

INPUT: 2MRS positions/redshifts, AllWISE Source Catalog, CatWISE2020
FOR each 2MRS galaxy:
- Cone-search AllWISE and CatWISE2020 within 5"
- Select one "best" counterpart per catalog by:
(1) cleanest artifact flags; (2) smallest separation; (3) W1 profile-fit quality
- Adopt W1/W2 (baseline): prefer CatWISE if clean, else AllWISE if clean
- Adopt W3/W4 (constraints): AllWISE (retain W4 based on policy)
Apply artifact policy:
STRICT: W1 TO W4 ALL clean
LENIENT: W1 TO W3 clean AND W4 kept even if flagged (marked)
Compute colors; apply AGN/starburst masks:
- Stern: (W1-W2) >= 0.8 if W2 < 15.05
- Assef R90: (W1-W2) >= R90(W2)
- Jarrett wedge: domain + two oblique boundaries
Select retained sample (union mask if desired)
FOR each T in {150,200,300,400,600} K:
FOR each constraining band b in {W3,W4}:
- Form 3-sigma cap F_nu,b^cap using detections/non-detections consistently
- Convert to bolometric cap with WISE RSR: F_bol^(b)(T)
- L_wh^(b)(T) = 4*pi D_L^2 F_bol^(b)(T)
- L_wh^max(T) = min[L_wh^(W3)(T), L_wh^(W4)(T)]
Aggregate:
- Distributions of L_wh^max(T) (p50, p90, ...)
- N_eff(T, L_thr) = count[L_wh^max(T) <= L_thr]
- f_95(T, L_thr) ~= 3 / N_eff(T, L_thr) (zero-event upper bound)
ROBUSTNESS:
- Policy variants (STRICT/LENIENT), masks (none/Stern/Stern+Assef/union)
- RSR vs monochromatic at pivot, RJ subtraction ON/OFF
- Extended-source stress (+10% on limiting band)
