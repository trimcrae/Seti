                                                                                    Abstract


                                            We consider a consistent deformation of the (anti)holomorphic generating system of [1],
                                            aiming to provide a map to the (anti)holomorphic truncation of the Vasiliev theory. In our
                                            new formulation, the previously rigidly defined master-field Λ can be shifted by dz -exact
                                            projective one-forms. The class of projective one-forms is described comprehensively, and
                                            corresponding projective identities are proven. dz -exact forms of the order n in C induce
                                            field redefinition of order (n + 1) and beyond. Explicit expression for the (n + 1)-th order
                                            field redefinitions are provided. An analysis of the gauge transformation of the second
                                            order in C in zero-forms is performed, identifying necessary and sufficient conditions for
                                            the part of the field redefinition of the second order that can be gauged away. The field
                                            redefinitions induced by the shift of Λ are proven to be nontrivial.

1      Introduction
Higher spin gauge theory is a field theory describing gauge interactions between gauge fields of
all spins. Theories possessing such a large symmetry algebra are argued to describe interactions
at ultra-high energies [2] when all symmetries become manifest. Also, due to the large sym-
metry algebra and hence the infinite spectrum, such theories find applications in holography
[3] and cosmology [4]. Despite numerous yet unsolved problems like HS symmetry breaking
(see, for example, [5], [6], [7], [8], [9] for recent attempts), there is still an unresolved question
regarding locality. There are early observations that show that HS theory cannot be a local
field theory in the traditional sense; the number of derivatives in interaction vertices grows with
spin indefinitely [10], [11], [12], [13]. On the other hand, it is precisely the HS algebra and its
spectrum that allow us to cancel infinities in pioneering quantum models [14], [15].
    While the traditional notion of locality is not applicable, a different one was developed, the
so-called spin-locality. Such locality implies that for a given set of spins, the interaction vertex
contains only a finite number of derivatives. It is worth highlighting that the particular local
functional class to which the vertices belong is vital for the consistent formulation of the theory.
Constraining the functional class for the vertices, one also constrains possible field redefinitions
and thus the remaining predictability of the theory.
    There are many approaches to HS dynamics available in the literature (see recent reviews
[16], [17], [18] and references therein). We confine ourselves to the so-called unfolded approach
[19], [20] (see [21],[22],[23] for recent applications of the formalism) and to the formalism of
generating systems originally proposed by Vasiliev in [24] (see also [25]). The Vasiliev generating
system allows us to write down the HS dynamics in the form

    Up to field redefinitions, η and η̄ are the only coupling constants of the theory [24]. Vertices
Υ•• (•, . . . , •) on the r.h.s. of (1.1), (1.2) are proportional to the powers of η and η̄ indicated
in the superscript. Vertices proportional exclusively to the powers of η are called holomorphic;
similarly, vertices proportional to powers of η̄ are called antiholomorphic. Technically, in such
vertices, the contraction between dotted indices is described exclusively by the dotted part of
the product (1.5) in the holomorphic sector, and vice versa in the antiholomorphic one. For that
reason we omit writing barred variables explicitly and barred Moyal-product is implicit. The
counterpart to pure holomorphic and antiholomorphic vertices is the so-called mixed sector,
with vertices proportional to both η and η̄. This sector is far less investigated in the literature
compared to the (anti)holomorphic one; nonetheless, some results are available. Introducing a
milder notion of locality, the vertices Υηη̄ (ω, ω, C, C) and Υηη̄ (ω, C, C, C) were computed [33],

                                                             3
[37] (see also [38], where a different generating system was developed to attack the mixed sector
of HS theory). As shown in [25], HS theory admits a consistent truncation leaving only, for
example, holomorphic vertices non-trivial. Such theories are called self-dual HS theories or
chiral in the literature.
    The term generating system means that to obtain the equations (1.1), (1.2), one needs to
solve additional equations. These equations describe the extra dependence of the so-called
master-fields on the auxiliary variables z. The equations are designed in such a way that the
dependence on these auxiliary z-variables vanishes in the resulting dynamics, and only the
dependence on the ys survives in (1.1), (1.2).
    Freedom of (anti)holomorphic truncation was successfully utilized in [1] (see also [39], where
a similar generating system was designed to describe symmetric HS fields in d > 4), where a
generating system that produces only (anti)holomorphic vertices was proposed. The generated
vertices are proven to be local in all orders and were found explicitly [40] (see also [41] which is
based upon [42]). Recently, the (anti)holomorphic system was generalized to also encompass the
mixed sector [38], eventually covering the full HS dynamics. However, it is worth mentioning
that the computation of mixed-sector interaction vertices from [38] is not as straightforward
as that in [25]; namely, on the way to (1.1), (1.2) one faces an additional equation which is
absent in Vasiliev formulation. Nonetheless, it is worth mentioning that for the computation
of Υηη̄ (ω, C, C, C), this equation was proven to be satisfied identically.
    While there are several generating systems for HS dynamics, each with their own advantages
and yet unresolved problems, the connection between them is not clear. The only nontrivial ex-
act agreement available at the moment is for the vertices Υη (ω, C, C) [1], [43]; nonetheless, there
are some indirect indications that the systems are indeed related, based on the shift-symmetry
properties of the vertices [44]. It is argued in the literature that the self-dual system of [1] can be
obtained from the Vasiliev equations [24] by virtue of the β → −∞ limit, but such statements
are far from being rigorous. Even though, in general, one may obtain all (anti)holomorphic
interaction vertices (1.1), (1.2) in both systems and look for field redefinitions of ω and C that
relate them
                         ω → ω + fω (ω, C, . . . , C), C → C + fC (C, . . . , C)                   (1.7)
such an approach does not look very promising from a technical point of view. In the first
place, if two systems are related, then such a relation should be seen at the level of generating
systems, which suggests looking at some deformation of either generating system. In this paper,
we deform the (anti)holomorphic system proposed in [1]. Its original formulation insisted on a
rigid definition for some of its master-fields, namely Λ[C], which should be a fixed functional
of C. We relax this requirement, thus introducing a consistent deformation at the level of the
generating system. More specifically, we allow the master-field Λ to be an arbitrary solution to
the corresponding inhomogeneous linear equation within a class of projective one-forms (to be
defined later). This allows one to add various exact forms to a particular solution

                                                Λ′ = Λ[C] + dz ε,                                               (1.8)

where Λ[C] is a rigidly defined functional from the original paper and dz ε should belong to
the space of projective one-forms. We refer to (1.8) as a gauge transformation in z-space,
utilizing the traditional notion from electrodynamics1 . We would like to clarify an ambiguity

gauge transformations, i.e., gauge transformations of eqs. (1.1), (1.2). For the second order in
C, we found necessary and sufficient conditions for the part of the field redefinition that has
no effect on the vertices and can be gauged away, providing explicitly the corresponding gauge
parameter. It turns out that (1.8) indeed produces a nontrivial deformation. Deformations of
the kind (1.8) were originally conjectured in [43]; however, the full description of projective
one-forms was lacking at that time. Here we provide a description of this class2 in terms of a
simple generating expression, which allows for writing the corresponding projective identities
in a concise form, handy for practical computations. Moreover, dz -exactness of the shift (1.8)
can be equivalently put in the form of a single integral constraint on the corresponding measure
µ(ρ1 , ρ2 ). This measure captures all the degrees of freedom of the field redefinition induced by
the transformation (1.8).
     Practical interest in the search for the relation between the Vasiliev system and the system
of [1] is the following. Even though one manages to get spin-local vertices from the Vasiliev gen-
erating equations, each new step required more and more sophisticated techniques3 , in contrast,
pure Poincaré lemma works perfectly for the system of [1] in each order of the perturbation the-
ory. Similarly, an all-order analysis exclusively by virtue of the Poincaré lemma was performed
for the Vasiliev system [50]. Vertices obtained in this manner are non-local, which, however,
does not imply that something is wrong with the generating equations as it was incorrectly
interpreted in [51]. Thus, having two systems solved by virtue of the Poincaré lemma, it is
quite natural to look at the deformation of one that relates them, to utilize, for example, the
success of the self-dual formulation. As a preliminary step of this quest, one should look at the
current interaction, namely, at the vertices Υη (ω, C, C). In [2], by virtue of a field redefinition,
this particular vertex from the pure Poincaré lemma approach in the Vasiliev framework was
turned into a spin-local one. This vertex was extensively studied in the literature; for example,
in [52] it was shown that it produces the correct boundary correlation function without intro-

    The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we recall the formulation of the (anti)holomorphic
generating system of [1]. We also show how interaction vertices are extracted up to the second
order in C in zero-forms. In Section 3 we provide a description of the projective one-forms and
identify which of them are the exact ones, which is equivalent to a simple integral constraint
on the integration measure µ(ρ1 , ρ2 ). In Section 4 we utilize the knowledge about projective
one-forms, relaxing the rigid definition for Λ. We consider the gauge parameter ε (cf. (1.8))
linear in C and extract the corresponding vertices up to the second order in C in zero-forms.
Section 5 is devoted to field redefinitions. We consider the generic case and provide a simple
expression in terms of Λ[C] and ε[C] that relates the vertices obtained in Section 2 and Section
4. In Section 6, using the description of the dz -exact projective one-forms, we provide an ex-
plicit expression for the field redefinition in zero-forms induced by our deformation in terms of
µ(ρ1 , ρ2 ). We also show that such a deformation cannot be gauged away. Moreover, we show
that the field redefinition induced by our deformation admits a local form for specific choices of
the integration measures. The Conclusion contains a discussion of the obtained results as well
as some open questions. In Appendix A, we present the proof of the projective identities for
the one-forms described in Section 3. In Appendix B, we prove that the conditions promoted
in Section 6 for the gauge part of the field redefinition are indeed necessary and sufficient.

7         Conclusion
A consistent deformation of the generating system proposed in [1] is considered. Our defor-
mation relaxes the requirement for the master-field Λ to be a fixed functional of the C-field.
We allow Λ to be a solution to the corresponding equation within the class of the so-called
projective one-forms, which we denote as C1,P . This class was described in full generality in
terms of a simple generating expression (3.5) useful for practical computations. Belonging to
this class guarantees the absence of unwanted constraints (cf. (2.31)) due to the projective
identities (3.10), (3.11), which were found here. We study a solution to the equation for Λ
(4.3), which differs from the previous one by a shift with an exact form dz ε ∈ C1,P linear in C.
Such a deformation, compared to the original formulation, induces a field redefinition, which
was found explicitly in (5.17) for arbitrary admissible ε.
    Our approach to C1,P class allowed a quite explicit description of ε ∈ C0 such that dz ε ∈
  1,P
C . In terms of integral representations, a simple integral constraint on the corresponding
measure µ(ρ1 , ρ2 ) (3.17) suffices, which is equivalent to the vanishing of certain moments (6.19).
We presented the induced change of the field frame in terms of µ(ρ1 , ρ2 ) (6.6)–(6.8), which
completely governs all degrees of freedom of the deformation we introduce. Moreover, for
specific choices of µ(ρ1 , ρ2 ), the corresponding field redefinitions admit a local form.
    As anticipated, certain ε do not produce an effect on vertices, inducing a gauge transfor-
mation instead. Those parameters are purely y-dependent functions ε0 (y) ∈ C0 . We identified
necessary and sufficient conditions (6.12)-(6.14) for the change of a field frame that corresponds
to a pure gauge. We thus conclude that the z-dependent part of ε ∈ C0 , namely ε1 (6.1), induces
a nontrivial deformation which cannot be gauged away.
