                                                                           ABSTRACT
                               This is a catalog of magnetic stars with a compilation of numerous observations. The analysis and
                           discussion of the results will be taken up in a later paper. The observations have been made over a pe-
                           riod of 11 years with the 100-inch and 200-inch telescopes, using an analyzer for the Zeeman effect.
                               Table 1 lists repeated observations of 89 sharp-line stars. Magnetic polarity, field strength, and veloc-
                           ity are tabulated. Notes on spectral peculiarities and line widths are given. Table 2 lists 66 stars that
                           probably, but not definitely, show the Zeeman effect. Table 3 consists of 61 sharp-line stars not showing
                           evidence of a coherent magnetic field. Table 4 is a collection of 122 stars with lines too broad to permit
                           measurement.
                               Of the magnetic stars in Table 1, one is a cluster-type variable, 1 is a subdwarf, 2 are of type S, 3 are
                           M giants, 7 are “metallic-line” objects, and 70 are A-type stars with sharp or ultra-sharp lines. Stellar
                           magnetic fields are therefore probably ubiquitous. All stellar magnetic fields appear to be variable. Of
                           the A-type stars sufficiently observed, 5 are regular magnetic variables having periods near 1 week, very
                           large and nearly uniform magnetic amplitude, and nearly symmetrical reversal of polarity; about 22 are
                           irregular variables showing reversal of polarity; and about 15 are irregular variables showing always the
                           same polarity. The irregular variations prove that large-scale intrinsic hydromagnetic fluctuations occur
                           in the photospheric layers.

                              This paper is a compilation of observational results on stellar magnetic fields. It is
                           intended that in a later paper the data will be analyzed, discussed, and to some extent
                           interpreted. The measurements given here are based on spectrograms obtained since
                           1946 with the coudé spectrographs of the 100-inch telescope at Mount Wilson and the
                           200-inch telescope at Palomar Mountain. Plates were taken with a differential analyzer
                           for rotatory polarization due to the longitudinal Zeeman effect and were measured,
                           largely by Miss Sylvia Burd, for magnetic-field intensity, polarity, and radial velocity.
                              The principal results are listed in Table 1, which is a catalog of 89 stars, arranged in
                           order of right ascension. For these stars the presence of a coherent magnetic field is
                           indicated by the Zeeman effect. The co-ordinates of each star for 1950 are followed by its
                           visual magnitude, spectral type, and w, index of line width. Brief notes on the spectrum
                           and on the characteristics of the magnetic field are followed by a tabulation of observa-
                           tions of the star, giving plate number, calendar date (U.T.), effective magnetic-field
                           intensity, He, with polarity (+ or — ) and probable error, radial velocity, v, and remarks.
                           A positive polarity indicates that the field vector is toward the observer. For many
                           plates that have not been measured, only the magnetic polarity is indicated. Since
                           virtually all stellar magnetic fields are variable, most stars listed have been observed
                           repeatedly. Table 2 is a list of 66 stars which probably, but not definitely, have coherent
                           fields. Table 3 lists 61 stars with sharp or fairly sharp spectrum lines giving little or no
                           indication of the Zeeman effect. Finally, Table 4 is a rather heterogeneous collection of
                            122 stars, examined for various reasons, which have spectrum lines too broad to permit
                           measurement of the Zeeman effect.

                                                           MEASUREMENT OP MAGNETIC MELDS
                              The technique of observation and reduction has been outlined in earlier papers (Bab-
                           cock 1947; Babcock and Cowling 1953). The differential analyzer for rotatory polariza-
                           tion, which is placed directly in front of the slit, is designed for the detection of the longi-
                           tudinal Zeeman effect, since this will produce a mutual displacement between lines in

   In general, the aim is to measure thirty or more unblended metallic lines on each
plate, including a few of each prominent element in the neutral or ionized state. Usually
the effective field is computed for each element separately, as well as for the whole
spectrum, and the velocity (reduced to the sun) is derived as well.

the Zeeman splitting of spectrum lines amounts to only a small fraction of an angstrom
(the ratio is about 105 gauss/A). It is therefore essential to select stars having very sharp
lines if precise measurements are to be made. High dispersion and a high degree of
spectral purity and resolving power are also required. These are provided by the coudé
grating spectrographs with Schmidt cameras (Bowen, 1952). Where possible, a dis-
persion of 4.5 A/mm has been used. The equivalent slit-width is typically about 0.07 A.

                              A comparison of the internal probable errors of the magnetic-field intensities and of
                           the velocities may suggest at first glance that the latter are relatively larger than would
                           be expected. For example, in some ultra-sharp-line stars such as 10 Aquilae, the respec-
                           tive internal probable errors of measurement are about ±30 gauss and ±0.1 km/sec.
                           A p.e. of ± 30 gauss, derived from differential measures of the Zeeman effect of thirty
                           lines on a spectrogram of 4.5 A/mm dispersion, corresponds to ±0.0001 mm on the
                           plate, or ±0.007 km/sec in terms of equivalent velocity. The reason that this last figure
                           is more than an order of magnitude smaller than the actual p.e. derived for the velocity
                           lies largely in the lack of sufficient precision in the standard laboratory wave lengths of
                           stellar lines as listed. Wave lengths consistently accurate to +0.001 A or better are re-
                           quired to match the precision of measurement now regularly achieved with sharp-
                           line stellar spectra. Such precision is lacking for nearly all but a very limited number of
                           “Secondary Standards of Wave Length” measured with the interferometer and listed
                           by the Commission on Wave Lengths of the International Astronomical Union (H. D.
                           Babcock 1928). High precision of the magnetic results is further enhanced by the method
                           of differential measurement of contiguous line images for the Zeeman effect, with virtual-
                           ly no dependence on the comparison spectrum, except for initial alignment of the wire.

                                                              SELECTION OF STARS
                              This investigation has involved a sampling of sharp-line stars in various parts of the
                           Hertzsprung-Russell diagram. By far the most rewarding, from the standpoint of strong
                           magnetic fields, have been the A-type stars. Morgan (1935), in a classical study of the
                           spectra of class A, showed that a one-dimensional system fails to define these spectra
                           uniquely and that there is definite evidence for some physical variable in addition to
                           temperature and surface gravity. This group includes a number of spectrum variables
                           (Deutsch 1947). Virtually all the well-marked “peculiar” A-type stars that have suf-
                           ficiently sharp lines are found to have magnetic fields. Fields have also been detected in
                           several metallic-line stars (defined by Roman, Morgan, and Eggen 1948), in the cluster-
                           type variable RR Lyrae, and in a few red giants.
                              Since the strongest magnetic fields have been found among the sharp-line A-type
                           stars, this group has been intensively observed. An effort has been made to discover and
                           to test for the Zeeman effect all truly sharp-line stars of type A that are within reach.
                           This test is believed to be reasonably complete to a limit between the sixth and seventh
                           magnitudes and includes some as faint as the ninth magnitude. Various earlier catalogs
                           and investigations of A-type stars and their spectra, mostly with low dispersion, have
                           been indispensable in making up the observing list for the present investigation. The
                           Henry Draper Catalogue (Cannon and Pickering 1918-1924) has been the primary
                           source; others include Adams, Joy, Humason, and Brayton (1935), Adams and Joy
                           (1926), Bidelman and Böhm (1955), Deutsch (1947), Harper (1937), Morgan (1933),
                           Provin (1953), Roman (1949), Slettebak (1954), Walther. (1949), and Zirin (1951).
                           Many other astronomers, in correspondence or conversation, have suggested likely
                           stars to be tested for the Zeeman effect. In this respect, P. W. Merrill, J. L. Greenstein,
                           and C. Jaschek have been particularly helpful.

                                                                  LINE WIDTHS
                             In general, spectra having metallic lines with widths greater than about 0.3 A are
                           quite difficult to measure for the Zeeman effect. Strong fields can, however, sometimes

                           be detected even if the line widths are as great as 0.6 or 0.8 A. For those few stars having
                           line widths of about 0.1 A, the internal probable error of the effective magnetic field,
                           derived from about thirty lines on a single spectrogram, may be as low as 20 or 30 gauss.
                           The uncertainty of field-intensity determinations naturally increases rapidly with line
                           width. Most effort, therefore, has been devoted to a search for and measurement of stars
                           having line widths less than 0.3 A. For these, the Doppler broadening resulting from
                           axial rotation is so small as to be of the same order of magnitude as the Zeeman broaden-
                           ing.                     '        .
                              The rotational velocity, ve sin i, as measured by earlier investigators (e.g., Westgate
                           1933; Slettebak 1954) on plates of lower dispersion made with spectrographs of lower
                           resolving power, has proved to be inadequate as a guide to the selection of ultra-sharp-
                           line stars. Indeed, many discrepancies have become apparent when low-dispersion re-
                           sults were compared with line widths measured on high-resolution plates of 4.5 A/mm
                           dispersion. It appears that with plates having dispersions of the order of 50 A/mm it was
                           impossible adequately to measure line widths in the range up to at least 0.5 A, with
                           the result that the observed distribution function of effective rotational velocities, ve
                           sin i, was distorted for small values. Most of the published determinations of ve sin i
                           have depended upon measures of the width of the Mg n doublet at X 4481; yet high-
                           resolutión spectrograms, if the lines are ultra-sharp, show the doublet resolved into its
                           two components, 0.2 A apart (Fig. 2, d).
                              A detailed study of line widths in sharp-line stars, with high dispersion, would be a
                           major undertaking, not within the scope of the present work. It would be complicated
                           by diverse Zeeman broadening, velocity fields, and variable profiles. If carried out with
                           a microphotometer, it would require the utmost care to avoid loss of effective resolution.
                           Nonetheless, derivation of an “index of line width,” w, has proved valuable for most of
                           the sharp-line stars studied here. This is simply the extreme width in angstroms of
                           typical unblended metallic lines of moderate intensity, in the region near X 4200, as
                           measured directly on the plate with a comparator, minus the projected slit-width (usual-
                           ly 0.07 A). As a working parameter, this index has proved to be quite useful, although
                           subject to later improvement. Because attention is usually centered on nearly the same
                           spectral region and because the line broadening results from a mixture of effects, of
                           which the Doppler effect may be relatively unimportant, it is more appropriate to express
                           the line width in angstroms than in equivalent kilometers per second. The index w has
                           been listed in the heading for most stars in Table 1. Observations show that line widths
                           are variable in many of the magnetic stars.
                              In the spectra of some stars with strong magnetic fields, the line widths at times de-
                           pend on the sign of the circular analyzer with which the observations are made. Thus,
                           when right-handed and left-handed polarized spcetra are compared, lines in one may be
                           systematically sharper than those in the other. The sharpening may be specific with
                           respect to the element, to the stage of ionization, or even to particular multiplets; it also
                           shows some dependence on the Zeeman patterns of the lines. This effect, known as the
                           “crossover effect,” is usually seen in stars showing strong reversing fields, near the phases
                           of reversal (e.g., HD 71866, Babcock 1956). The sign of the crossover effect may be
                           taken as positive when the effective field polarity is changing from negative to positive.

                                                                 GENERAL REMARKS
                              While it is the primary purpose here merely to present observational data, leaving
                           analysis and discussion for the future, it may be remarked that stellar magnetic fields
                           are evidently ubiquitous. Only coherent fields, of one outstanding polarity, can be direct-
                           ly observed and measured by the means employed here. It is certainly to be expected
                           that a great many stars possess magnetic fields of mixed polarity, characterized by more
                           or less random distribution associated with turbulence. In such cases the Zeeman effect
                           leads only to a small degree of line broadening, almost impossible to distinguish from
                           other effects.
                              Of the magnetic stars in Table 1, one is a cluster-type variable, one is a subdwarf, two
                           are of type S, three are M-type giants, seven are íímetallic-line,, stars, and seventy are
                           A-type stars with “peculiar” spectra and sharp or ultra-sharp lines. Few A-type stars
                           with sharp lines do not show magnetic fields, and, of these few, virtually none has a
                           peculiar spectrum.
                              It is highly significant that all stellar fields appear to be variable. Of the A-type stars
                           sufficiently observed, five are regular magnetic variables having periods near 1 week,
                           very large and nearly uniform magnetic amplitude, and nearly symmetrical reversal of
                           polarity; about twenty-two are irregular or pseudo-regular magnetic variables showing
                           reversal of polarity; and about fifteen are irregular magnetic variables showing always
                           the same polarity. The irregular variations found in the majority of these stars can occur
                           only through large-scale intrinsic hydromagnetic fluctuations of the photospheric ma-
                           terial.
                              Of the magnetic A-type stars in Table 1, ten are spectroscopic binaries, of which the
                           following are believed to be newly identified: HD 8441, HR 710, 41 Tau, 3 Hya (?),
                           HD 9996, and HD 125248.

                              Measurement and reduction of the spectrograms have required much work of a
                           precise and exacting nature. A major share of this measuring has been most ably ac-
                           complished by Miss Sylvia Burd. Without her devotion to the task, this catalog
                           would not have reached its present proportions. Another large contribution, since 1951,
                           has been made by Mrs. A. J. Deutsch. Miss G. Gjellestad and Mr. J. R. Novotny have
                           done most of the measuring of plates of HD 153882 and HD 188041, respectively. Dr.
                           P. W. Merrill and Dr. A. J. Deutsch have read the manuscript and made constructive
                           suggestions. To all of these and to many others who contributed to this work I am deeply
                           grateful.

                                   Even among the magnetic stars, the spectrum of 21 Per is extraordinary. As Morgan
                                pointed out (1935): “In this one spectrum are incorporated most of the peculiarities
                                known among the A-stars. The Si n doublet is strong, both Mn n and Eu n are well
                                marked, Srn X4078 and X4215 are unusually intense, and the unidentified line at
                                X 4200 is fairly strong.” Lines of Fe i are quite weak.
                                   With high dispersion, most of the lines, including X 4481, appear shallow, having the
                                appearance of dilution.1 Considerable line broadening is present. Presumably this is due
                                largely to axial rotation; yet there seems to be some diversity in line widths. The lines
                                X 4201 and X 4205 seem particularly wide. There are subtle variations in some of the
                                line profiles. For example, changes in the width and symmetry of X4213 have been
                                noted, and on one plate this line appears double (Pb 735). On Pb 1894, Feu X4233
                                appears to have weak emission wings. While one suspects that definite variations in
                                equivalent width might be established by microphotometry, no variations sufficient to
                                identify the star as a typical spectrum variable have been detected.
                                   Owing to the width and shallowness of the lines, measurements of the Zeeman effect
                                are not easy; yet the presence of a rather strong and variable magnetic field, showing
                                reversal of polarity, was readily established. The extremes of He are about —1270 and
                                + 1350 gauss. Rather rapid changes have been found; for example, He was —1270,
                                +650, and +1000 gauss on December 19, 20, and 21, 1953. No regularity has been
                                found in the field variations. There is often a considerable diversity in the field intensity
                                indicated by various elements; divergence has been noted particularly for Cr n, Si n,
                                and Ti n, and less certainly for Eu n and Mn n. This star is rather similar to HD 173650
                                (No. 65).
                                   One plate in the visual region resulted in a large probable error because of unusual
                                grain and the fact that only eight lines were measurable. The interstellar D lines were
                                extremely sharp.

65. ED 173650
18h43m28s, +21°55'      6.4   AOp    0.2 < w < 0.4:    \J
   This is an extraordinary irregular magnetic variable that shows a fluctuating field of
reversible polarity and is also a spectrum variable that may be irregular. There is only a
loose correlation, if any, of the varying line intensities with the magnetic variations.
The metallic-line spectrum (especially Sr n) is usually stronger when the field shows
negative polarity, but there are exceptions to this (Ce 8281, Pb 2570, 2579).
   This star is in some respects similar to 21 Per (No. 9); for example, lines of Si n and
Sr ii are prominent, while Mn n, Cr i, Cr n, Eu n, Gd n, and X 4201 are seen. Intensity
variations of the lines of Sr n are well marked, and the majority of the lines of the
metals, including Eu n, vary with Sr n. In general, the profiles are shallow and the line
widths diverse. The K line is sharp-edged and “square.” On plates showing a stronger
metallic-line spectrum (Pb 1453, 1465, 1471) many of the lines tend to assume a flat-
bottomed profile, and many show weak, narrow emission at the edges of the absorption
profile. On a number of spectrograms, various elements indicate anomalous values for
the magnetic-field intensity. Cm, Sin, and probably Mnn are noteworthy in this
respect.
   The observations may not rule out the possibility that the line-intensity variations
are regular, with a period of the order of 10 days. The magnetic variations, however,
seem quite irregular. (It must be remembered that, because of the divergent values
yielded by different elements, the mean He depends heavily on the choice of lines.) In a
1-day interval (August 10-11, 1952) He changed from —400 to +480 gauss, while in a
3-day interval (June 27-30,1953) it changed gradually from —100 to +450 gauss. These
and other irregularities show that large intrinsic hydromagnetic fluctuations can and do
occur at the surface of the star. A velocity range of 4 km/sec is indicated.

68. RR Lyme
19h23m528,  +42°41'
   This type-star of the cluster variables has been extensively studied by many investi-
gators. A bibliography is given by Detre (1943; see also Struve 1947, and Münch and
Terrazas 1946). The period of light-variation is 0.567 day.
   A test for the Zeeman effect was made in 1950, but definite evidence of the presence of
a reversing magnetic field was first found in 1955 (Babcock 1955). Extremes of He, as
measured, are +1170 and —1580, with probable errors of about +150 gauss. The field
shows significant changes in intervals of a few hours, but values of He, when plotted
according to the elements of light-variation, did not define a smooth curve. In June,
1956, several plates yielded weaker field intensities than had been obtained earlier, sug-
gesting that both short- and long-term field variations occur.
   The coherent magnetic field of RR Lyr is significant for the theory of hydromagnetics
because this is a known pulsating star and for the theory of stellar evolution because of
the critical position of the RR Lyr variables in the evolutionary scheme of population II
objects.
