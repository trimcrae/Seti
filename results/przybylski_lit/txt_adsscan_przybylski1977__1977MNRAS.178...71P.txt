                                        Is iron present in the atmosphere of HD 101065?




                                        A. Przybylski Mount Stromlo and Siding Spring Observatory, Private Bag,
                                        Woden PO, Australian Capital Territory 2606, Australia



                                        Received 1976 May 26; in original form 1975 June 16




                                        Summary. In spite of the coincidence of many stellar lines with laboratory
                                        wavelengths of iron lines no convincing proof for the presence of iron in
                                        HD 101065 is found. The absence of several strong lines of iron in the spec-
                                        trum proves that iron is absent or highly underabundant in the star. The
                                        underabundance of iron is at least 2.4 in the logarithm.

                         1 Introduction

                         Peculiar A stars as a rule have a spectrum of a normal star on which some additional features
                         are superimposed. However, the star HD 101065 proved to be an exception to this. It is a
                         truly peculiar star since — apart from hydrogen lines — it has hardly any spectral features in
                         common with normal stars.
                            The most striking feature of HD 101065 is the absence of iron peak elements in its spec-
                         trum. However, this was not yet obvious in 1961, when only low-dispersion spectra were
                         available and consequently some incorrect statements were made in the first paper announc-
                         ing the discovery of the star (Przybylski 1961): for instance the radial velocity of 2 km s"1,
                         obtained from lines wrongly attributed to iron and iron peak elements, was not correct.
                            The Coudé spectrogram of the dispersion of 6.7 Âmm-1 obtained in 1961 May showed
                         that its investigation requires caution and that lack of any preconceived ideas is a necessary
                         condition for success. The spectrum was measured with the help of an impersonal setting
                         device designed by Gollnow (1962). About 3000 features were recorded in an interval from
                         3650 to 4830 Â. This means that the average distance between two neighbouring features is
                         about 0.4 Â. The mutual blending of lines is very severe and unblended lines hardly exist. In
                         fact it can be said that the whole spectrum in the photographic region is one large blend.
                         Obviously the risk of misidentification of spectral lines in such a crowded spectrum is very
                         high.

                         3 Probability functions for the coincidence of lines

                         Easy as it was to identify the rare earths the search for elements which dominate the spectra
                         of normal stars proved to be an extremely difficult task. Since the mean distance between
                         two neighbouring features is only 0.4 Â it was almost always possible to find a star line near
                         the position corresponding to the laboratory wavelength of any element but hardly any
                         correlation could be noticed between the intensities in HD 101065 and in the Sun or the
                         stars. The lack of this correlation is especially obvious for lines of neutral iron which is
                         represented in the Sun by several strong lines up to a width of 3 Â. Since several strong lines
                         of neutral iron are absent or at best weak, the conclusion that iron is practically absent is the
                         only logical one. In this case the assumption must be made that some stellar lines coinciding
                         with iron lines are in fact lines due to the rare earths, in some cases not yet included in the
                         published lists of laboratory wavelengths.
                             This radical conclusion was influenced to a certain extent by the extraordinary faintness
                         of the K line of ionized calcium, which can be judged from the reproduction of the spectrum
                         published by Jones, Wolff & Bonsack (1974).
                             The high deficiency of calcium is not in itself proof of the deficiency of another neigh-
                         bouring element and therefore the evidence for the absence of each particular element must
                         be examined separately.
                             The theoretical principles for the identification of lines in a spectrum were discussed by
                         Russell & Bowen (1929). In their analysis they assumed that an element is present in the
                         spectrum if the number of coincidences between its laboratory wavelengths and the
                         measured lines of the spectrum substantially exceeds the number of accidental coincidences.
                             The formulae derived by Russell & Bowen are based on the assumption that lines in the
                         spectrum are distributed at random. This means that small intervals between the lines are
                         possible, and should be more numerous than large intervals. In practice, however, two close
                         lines cannot be recorded separately and they form one single blend. On the other hand, in a
                         real spectrum the intervals do not exceed a certain maximum value, while in a random

                             The present investigations show that sometimes it is impossible to decide whether or not

                         account. In such cases the correlation between the intensities of stellar lines and the corre-

                         spectrum.

                         5 Search for lines of neutral iron

                         The conclusion reached by the author that several elements normally present in stars are
                         missing in HD 101065 was challenged recently by Wegner & Petford (1974). For this reason
                         the available evidence for or against the presence of several elements was re-examined and the
                         present paper is devoted exclusively to the most important element, iron.
                            Since the presence of iron in the spectrum is doubtful, only the strongest lines of iron
                         were included in the investigations. Obviously it makes little sense to ‘prove’ the presence of
                         some faint lines in the spectrum if the presence of strong lines is doubtful. For this reason in
                         the region above 3800 Â only lines with an equivalent width exceeding 150mÂ in the Sun
                         were taken into account and below 3800 Â, where the spectrum is very crowded, only lines
                         exceeding 500 mÂ. Blends of two iron lines of similar strength were disregarded since the
                         wavelength of blends introduces an element of uncertainty into the investigations. In all 91
                         strong iron Unes were selected. In this sample nine lines had to be excluded because they fall
                         in the regions of broad blends, where it is very difficult to draw any conclusions about the
                         presence of any elements. Thus 82 lines were left.
                             Of the 82 lines, 11 have equivalent widths over lOOOmA in the Sun, nine between 700
                         and 1000 mÂ, and 18 between 500 and 700 mÂ. The remaining 44 lines have equivalent
                         widths between 150 and 500 mÂ.

                             In F stars strong lines of iron have low residual intensities. For this reason local intensity
                         peaks between the lines of the rare earths would be obliterated by strong iron lines coincid-
                         ing with them if the abundance of iron in HD 101065 were normal or nearly normal. The
                         presence of sharp intensity peaks in positions, where one would expect a strong iron line
                         (Group A), is, therefore, a strong argument against the presence of iron in any substantial
                         amount in the star. Faint distortions of such intensity peaks, if attributed to iron, may
                         possibly be used for the determination of the upper limit of the abundance of iron in the
                         star. However, in most cases such distortions do not exceed possible effects of irregularities
                         in the density of photographic grains.
                             In a similar way the lines of Group B can be used as an argument against the presence of
                         iron in any substantial amount in the atmosphere of the star.
                             The lines of this Group C can be explained reasonably well as lines of the rare earths; they
                         cannot be considered as positive proof for the existence of iron in the star. On the other
                         hand,*the possibility cannot be excluded that iron contributes to a certain extent to the lines
                         of this group. However, if a strong line of iron coincides with a weak stellar line, one can
                         conclude that at best iron is underabundant. A few such lines can be found in this group.

                         7 The upper limit for iron abundance

                         The investigations described in the previous section can be used to determine the upper
                         limit for the abundance of iron. However, some simplifying assumptions must be made.
                            The adopted ionization temperature of the reversing layer is          = 6000 K (flj = 0.84).
                         This is the temperature at the mean optical depth r = 0.15 for a model stellar atmosphere
                         with the effective temperature 6500 K, equal to the temperature of the continuum of
                         HD 101065. The difference between the ionization temperature and the excitation tempera-
                         ture is unknown. In the present calculations it was assumed that there is no difference
                         between those two temperatures. The adopted electron pressure is logpe = 1.0. The con-
                         tinuous opacities were taken from Bode’s (1965) tables. The ^/values of May, Richter &
                         Wichelmann (1974) supplemented by the values of Garz & Kock (1969) were used in
                         computations.
                            In order to eliminate the effect of systematic errors in the ¿/values and in the atmospheric
                         parameters, a comparison was made of the results obtained for Procyon and for HD 101065.
                            The same atmospheric parameters were adopted for Procyon as for HD 101065. A
                         logarithmic abundance of 7.1 was obtained from lines of neutral iron if a logarithmic abun-
                         dance of 12.0 is adopted for hydrogen. This value is practically independent of the adopted
                         electron pressure. However, it is necessary to lower the logarithm of the electron pressure to
                         0.4 in order to obtain the same abundance from lines of singly ionized iron.
                            An upper limit for the iron abundance can be found by making some plausible assump-
                         tions about the strength of iron lines in the spectrum.
                            It can be readily assumed that several lines of Groups A and B, for instance the lines at
                         X3820, 3825, 3834 and 4045 Â, have equivalent widths not exceeding 50 mÂ. Of all these

197 7MNRAS.178. . .71P

                         lines the first one leads to the lowest abundance, 3.4 in the logarithm, which is an under-
                         abundance of 3.7 in comparison with Procyon.
                             For several lines coinciding with local intensity peaks it is possible to set an upper limit
                         for the depth of a hypothetical iron line. Then using an appropriate depth versus equivalent
                         width relation we can determine the upper limit for the equivalent widths of such lines.
                             It is extremely difficult to find this relation for HD 101065. For this reason the correla-
                         tion found for Procyon was also adopted for HD 101065. Using the appropriate graph we
                         derive the equivalent width of 104mÂ for the line at X 3820 Â which has a depth of 30 per

                         abundance of 3.1 in comparison with Procyon. The line at X3859 Â for which an equivalent
                         width of 176 mÂ can be adopted leads to an underabundance of 2.6 and nine other lines
                         give an underabundance of 2.2 with a scatter not exceeding 0.1.
                             The result obtained from the possible depth of the line at X3820Â may be subject to
                         some criticism for several reasons.
                             Firstly , although no serious error in the adopted continuum can be expected the possi-
                         bility still exists that it should be raised. However, it is very unlikely that it should be raised
                         by more than 10 per cent. In this case the equivalent width of the line should be increased to
                         155 mÂ and, consequently, the underabundance of iron should be lowered by 0.5 to 2.6.
                             Secondly, the adopted ionization temperature         = 6000 K may be too low if the redden-
                         ing correction obtained in previous investigations (Przybylski 1977) is too low. An error of up
                         to 200 K in the temperature of the star is possible and for this reason the calculations were
                         repeated for       = 6267 K which was adopted by Wegner & Petford in their investigations. It
                         was found that the abundance of iron was increased by 0.25 as the result of the change in
                         the ionization temperature.
                             The combined effect of both changes-in the position of the continuum and in the
                         temperature — is the increase of abundance by 0.7 from 4.0 to 4.7 and the corresponding
                         decrease of the underabundance by the same amount from 3.1 to 2.4 in the logarithm.
                             This result may not be valid if a strange source of opacity is present in the atmosphere in
                         HD 101065. However, this is a very remote possibility. The analysis of the spectrum
                         (Przybylski 1966) shows that the chemical composition of the atmosphere is simpler than
                         that of a normal star and that the atmosphere is probably metal poor. Hence, one would not
                         expect any strange, new sources of opacity to be present in the atmosphere of the star.
                             The results obtained in this section are independent of the electron pressure as long as the
                         negative hydrogen ion is the main source of opacity. In a late type F star this should be the
                         case.

                         9 Conclusions
                         The obvious conclusion from the search for iron lines in the spectrum is that this element is
                         absent or at least underabundant in the atmosphere of HD 101065.
                             The present results cannot be reconciled with the conclusions of Wegner & Petford
                         (1974) who find a nearly normal abundance of iron in the star. At the request of the author,
                         Wegner & Petford made some tests and included the results of the tests in their paper. They
                         claim that the tests confirm their findings and that the probability of an error in their con-
                         clusion is less than 0.001.
                             However, the paper of Wegner & Petford contains some data which cast some doubt on
                         the correctness of their conclusions. They state: ‘elements were considered possible contri-
                         butors to a line if they were either a rare earth or strong in the Sun’. The first part of this
                         rule is certainly correct but the second part is not necessarily so especially if one takes into
                         account that the published lists of rare earth lines are not complete and that about half of
                         the stellar lines cannot be identified at all. Thus the adopted criterion for the identification
                         of lines seems to be too lax.
                             The criterion adopted by Wegner & Petford obviously leads to the identification of lines
                         belonging to Group C in Section 5 as lines of the rare earths. On the other hand lines
                         belonging to Group D are wrongly identified as iron lines. Apparently not enough attention
                         was paid by Wegner & Petford to lines belonging to Groups A and B which are either absent
                         or at best abnormally weak. A critical examination of the maximum possible strength of
                         lines belonging to these groups in Section 7 shows that the abundance of iron cannot exceed
                         4.7 in the logarithm. The underabundance of iron in comparison with Procyon is then 2.4.
                            The result obtained in the present investigations obviously applies only to the visible
                         the star. In all probability the underabundance of iron is higher and the result obtained by
                         assuming that the equivalent width of the line at X3820Â does not exceed 50 mÂ is more
                         realistic. In fact, one cannot rule out the possibility that even this value is too low. Unfortu-
                         nately, at present a precise determination of the underabundance of iron is not possible.
                             The result obtained in the present investigations obviously applies only to the visible
                         outermost layer of the star. Nothing can be said about the chemical composition of the
                         deeper layers.
