Axion Quality Problem and Non-Minimal Gravitational Coupling

Abstract

In axion models, the global U(1) Peccei-Quinn (PQ) symmetry is explicitly broken by non-perturbative effects of gravity, such as axionic wormholes. The gravitational violation of the PQ symmetry due to wormholes is large enough to invalidate the PQ mechanism, which is entitled as the axion quality problem. Recently, a novel solution to this quality problem was suggested, where the non-minimal coupling of the axion field to gravity ξ𝜉\xi is introduced to suppress the wormhole contribution. In this work, we revisit the problem in a different but equally valid formulation of gravity, namely the Palatini formulation, where the Ricci scalar is solely determined by connection. We first find the axionic wormhole solution in the Palatini formulation, taking the full dynamical radial mode as well as the axial mode, then show that the quality problem is still resolved with the non-minimal coupling ξ𝜉\xi. The requested lower bound of ξ𝜉\xi in the Palatini formulation turns out to be slightly higher than that in the metric formulation.

I Introduction

The axion Weinberg (1978); Wilczek (1978), which is a pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson associated with the spontaneous breaking of the global U(1) Peccei-Quinn (PQ) symmetry Peccei and Quinn (1977a, b), is introduced as a solution to the strong CP problem. Below the QCD scale, the axion field obtains a periodic potential induced by QCD instantons to settle down to an exactly CP-conserving vacuum. However, even a tiny violation of this symmetry can jeopardize the PQ solution to the strong CP problem, leading to the mechanism being extremely sensitive to the quality of the PQ symmetry Dine (2022).

Indeed, it is expected that gravity may spoil any global symmetry at the least Kallosh et al. (1995); Banks and Seiberg (2011); Witten (2018); Harlow and Ooguri (2019, 2021) and the PQ symmetry may not be an exception. This violation of the U(1) symmetry generally displaces the axion field from the CP-conserving minimum of the axion potential. If the deviation is too large, the induced value of the neutron electric dipole moment becomes incompatible with the experimental upper bound Abel et al. (2020), and hence the PQ mechanism does not work as a viable solution to the strong CP problem. This is called the ‘axion quality problem’ Kamionkowski and March-Russell (1992); Holman et al. (1992); Barr and Seckel (1992) and there have been many possible solutions suggested in the literature Witten (1984); Randall (1992); Cheng and Kaplan (2001); Izawa et al. (2002); Choi (2004); Lillard and Tait (2018).

Meanwhile, an axionic wormhole Giddings and Strominger (1988a); Lee (1988); Kallosh et al. (1995), which is a gravitational instanton in Euclidean spacetime, is a well-known case that explicitly shows the gravitational violation of the U(1) symmetry. It has a non-trivial topology characterized by a global U(1) charge and induces effective local operators that violate the U(1) symmetry through computing quantum transition amplitudes in a semi-classical approximation Coleman (1988); Giddings and Strominger (1988b); Rey (1989); Abbott and Wise (1989). In this case, the field value of f𝑓f stays near the Planck scale at the throat of the wormhole, and the size of the wormhole also becomes close to the Planck length. The wormhole action now scales logarithmically as S∼n​log⁡(MP/fa)similar-to𝑆𝑛subscript𝑀𝑃subscript𝑓𝑎S\sim{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}n}\log(M_{P}/f_{a}) and it cannot grow sufficiently. Therefore, we still suffer from the quality problem Kallosh et al. (1995); Alonso and Urbano (2019); Alvey and Escudero (2021).

A recent work Hamaguchi et al. (2022) suggested a novel solution to the quality problem even in the presence of the dynamical radial field, by introducing a non-minimal gravitational coupling of a complex scalar field, ξ𝜉\xi. It is found that the quality problem is avoided for ξ≳2×103greater-than-or-equivalent-to𝜉2superscript103\xi\gtrsim 2\times 10^{3} having a sufficiently large wormhole action. We take this solution seriously, as the non-minimal coupling ξ𝜉\xi is allowed in any effective theory as long as the term is consistent with the symmetries of the theory. Indeed, the implications of the term have been widely explored in inflationary cosmology Futamase and Maeda (1989); Bezrukov and Shaposhnikov (2008); Park and Yamaguchi (2008); Bauer and Demir (2008); Tamanini and Contaldi (2011); Hertzberg (2010); Hamada et al. (2014, 2015); Jinno et al. (2019, 2020); Cheong et al. (2022a, b).

One potential loophole of the suggested solution in Ref. Hamaguchi et al. (2022) is noted: gravity can be equally valid when formulated in different ways. Indeed, distinctively from the conventional metric formulation taken in Ref. Hamaguchi et al. (2022), we can alternatively choose the Palatini formulation of gravity Einstein (1925); Ferraris et al. (1982) that takes the connection as an independent degree of freedom apart from the metric. While both the metric and Palatini formulations are equivalent within the so-called minimal gravity model of the Einstein-Hilbert action, the equivalence is generally broken in non-minimal models Ferraris et al. (1994); Magnano and Sokolowski (1994). Since we are not able to distinctively rule out either formulation at the current stage of our experimental knowledge, in this paper, we pursue to reconsider the Palatini formulation and examine if the noble solution to the quality problem remains valid. 222The intrinsic difficulty of the experimental probes becomes more transparent when one considers the Einstein frame. In this frame, the gravity sector becomes canonical in both formulations of gravity and all effects are recast to the scalar potential deformation, which non-minimally couples to the Ricci scalar in the Jordan frame. As long as we are not able to probe scalar field values large enough for the deformation of the potential to be significant, f≳MP/ξgreater-than-or-equivalent-to𝑓subscript𝑀𝑃𝜉f\gtrsim M_{P}/\xi, the differences in observables become negligible. On the other hand, for f​(R)𝑓𝑅f(R) gravity, there could be constraints for Palatini formulation coming from the fact that it violates the equivalence principle Olmo (2007); Wu et al. (2018).

IV Conclusion and discussions

In this work, we have discussed the effect of the non-minimal coupling to gravity ξ𝜉\xi on the axion quality problem in the Palatini formulation. In this formulation, the affine connection and the metric are independent a priori. Hence, for non-zero ξ𝜉\xi, the affine connection is different from the Levi-Civita connection of metric formulation, and so are the physical consequences compared to the usual metric formulation.

We have found that the presence of additional terms in the affine connection does affect the wormhole solution, resulting in a smaller wormhole action compared to that in the metric formulation. As a result, a larger value of the non-minimal coupling, ξ≳1×104greater-than-or-equivalent-to𝜉1superscript104\xi\gtrsim 1\times 10^{4}, is required in order to avoid the quality problem. We have also checked that our calculation does not violate the perturbative unitarity of the theory with large non-minimal coupling thanks to large radial field values at the wormhole throat.

More specifically, as ξ𝜉\xi increases, the wormhole throat decreases at first while the wormhole action value increases. For larger ξ𝜉\xi near the induced gravity limit, there is a rapid convergence to the Gidding-Stronminger solution. This coincidence happens in both the metric and Palatini formulations, and could be understood as a decoupling between the axion θ𝜃\theta and the radial mode f𝑓f, as explicitly shown in the Einstein frame.

Lastly, as mentioned in the introduction, a scalar field with a large non-minimal coupling is sometimes considered in the context of inflation which gives a consistent fit to CMB observations. In our case, the radial field f𝑓f can play the role of the inflaton.
