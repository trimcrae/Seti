# Target journals

Written 2026-09-11.  Scope statements and precedents are from the journals'
own pages and from the papers this manuscript cites; article-processing
charges and turnaround figures change and should be re-checked on the
journal's site at submission.

## Main paper (`paper/goo/`): "Grey goo as a technosignature"

~9,000 words, 6 figures, 4 tables, a Bayesian inference section, and a
released code package.  The paper is astronomy-facing (propagation physics,
dust dynamics, survey constraints) with a risk-analysis conclusion.

### 1. International Journal of Astrobiology (Cambridge University Press) — recommended

* **Fit.** IJA is where every nearest neighbour of this paper was published:
  Stevens, Forgan & O'Malley-James 2016 (observational signatures of
  self-destructive civilisations, the paper that lists grey goo); Ellery 2022a
  and 2022b (self-replicating probes as imminent, and how to curb their
  fruitfulness); Nicholson & Forgan 2013 (self-replicating probe exploration
  timescales); Bjørk 2007 (probe exploration); Osmanov 2020 (von Neumann
  micro-probes); Olson 2017 (visible galaxy-spanning civilisations).  A
  referee pool that has already reviewed those papers exists here and
  nowhere else.
* **Scope.** The journal covers "the origin, evolution, distribution and
  future of life in the universe", explicitly including SETI and the Fermi
  paradox; Fermi-paradox modelling papers with Bayesian content are a
  recurring type.
* **Format.** Research articles with no hard length limit at the scale of this
  manuscript; Cambridge's standard peer review; hybrid open access (gold OA
  optional, with a waiver/discount programme for authors at institutions with
  Cambridge read-and-publish agreements).  Indexed by ADS, Scopus and Web of
  Science.
* **Risk.** IJA's impact factor is modest and its audience is the astrobiology
  community rather than the risk community; the risk conclusion will reach
  that community through the companion paper instead.

### 2. Acta Astronautica (Elsevier) — alternative

* **Fit.** Armstrong & Sandberg 2013 ("Eternity in six hours") is the
  cosmological-reach precedent, and Acta Astronautica has a long record of
  interstellar-probe and SETI papers through the IAA SETI Permanent Committee.
  The right venue if the referees want the cosmological reach and the probe
  engineering foregrounded over the astrophysics of the residue.
* **Format.** Full-length articles; hybrid OA with an APC in the US$3,000
  range if gold OA is chosen; indexed by ADS, Scopus and Web of Science.
* **Risk.** Engineering readership; the Bayesian and anthropic sections may be
  asked to be shortened.

### 3. The Astronomical Journal / The Astrophysical Journal (AAS) — if the referee
report from IJA recommends a higher-profile venue

* **Fit.** Carroll-Nellenback et al. 2019 (AJ) and Hanson et al. 2021 (ApJ)
  are Fermi-paradox modelling papers the AAS journals accepted; Wright et al.
  2014 (Ĝ I and II, ApJ), Griffith et al. 2015 (ApJS) and Lacki 2025 (ApJ)
  are the survey and residue papers this manuscript builds on.  The dust-
  dynamics and residue-lifetime results are the parts an AAS referee would
  weigh.
* **Format.** AAS journals are fully open access with page-based charges (of
  order US$1,000 for a paper of this length); ADS-native.
* **Risk.** The risk-analysis conclusion and the anthropic material are
  outside the usual AAS scope and would likely be asked to be cut to a
  paragraph; the paper's point would survive but its audience would not
  include the people who elicit the risk numbers.

### Not recommended

* **Astrobiology (Mary Ann Liebert).** Publishes SETI and technosignature
  work (e.g. the NASA technosignature workshop reports) but has been less
  receptive to Fermi-paradox modelling than IJA; its APC for OA is high.
* **MNRAS.** Published Hephaistos I/II and Kennedy & Wyatt; would be a fit for
  the residue-lifetime physics alone but not for the inference and risk
  sections.
* **Journal of Cosmology.** Not a peer-reviewed venue in any meaningful sense;
  publication there would damage the paper.
* **MDPI Universe / Life.** Fast and open but with a reputational discount in
  the SETI community that this paper does not need to carry.

## Companion paper (`paper/replicators/`): "Dying of one's own replicators"

~4,000 words, one table, perspective format.

### 1. Futures (Elsevier) — recommended

* **Fit.** Futures has published the existential-risk literature this paper
  speaks to: Umbrello & Baum 2018 (net societal impacts of atomically precise
  manufacturing), the Baum et al. papers on long-term trajectories, and the
  "Foresight for Fermi" material; its readership is the risk and foresight
  community that elicits the probabilities the paper maps through.
* **Format.** Regular articles and shorter "perspectives"/essays; hybrid OA;
  Scopus and Web of Science indexed (not ADS).

### 2. Risk Analysis (Society for Risk Analysis / Wiley) — alternative

* **Fit.** Ćirković, Sandberg & Bostrom 2010 ("Anthropic shadow") is the
  formal basis of the paper's epistemic section and was published here; the
  journal takes perspective pieces on catastrophic and existential risk.
* **Risk.** Referees will want the argument formalised further than a
  perspective allows; a longer version with a worked decision-theoretic
  section would suit.

### 3. International Journal of Astrobiology — if the two papers are to be kept
together

Submitting both to IJA keeps one referee pool and one publication timeline; the
cost is that the companion's risk audience is not IJA's.
