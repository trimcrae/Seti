                                               ABSTRACT
                                               We study the local dark matter distribution in two models for the Milky Way (MW)-Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) interaction.
                                               The eﬀect of the LMC on the local dark matter distribution is dependent on the evolution of the MW-LMC system, such that
                                               a static model is insuﬃcient to accurately model the dark matter velocity distribution in the solar neighbourhood. An evolved
                                               model boosts local LMC dark matter particle velocities by nearly 50%, to a median value of ≈ 750 km s−1 . MW dark matter
                                               particles also experience a velocity boost caused by disc reﬂex motion owing to the infall of the LMC. We study the implications
                                               of LMC particles in the solar neighbourhood for dark matter detection experiments. The directionality of LMC particles is
                                               distinguishable from the MW particles, with a diﬀerence in the apparent origin centroid location between the MW and LMC
                                               particles of 26 ± 6◦ . This unique identiﬁer, along with their high velocities, can be utilised by directional detectors to search for
                                               dark matter particles originating in the LMC.
                                               Key words: Magellanic Clouds – Galaxy: kinematics and dynamics – dark matter – solar neighbourhood – Galaxy: halo
