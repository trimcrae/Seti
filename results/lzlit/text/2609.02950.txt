                                            The XENONnT Collaboration recently reported a new measurement of the solar pp neutrino flux via neutrino-
                                         electron elastic scattering using a multi-ton-scale liquid xenon detector (preprint submitted on Aug. 29, 2026 [1] and
                                         release talk on Aug. 31, 2026 [2]). While we congratulate them on this definitive result, we comment here on an
                                         important omission in their summary plot (Fig. 6).
arXiv:2609.02950v1 [hep-ex] 1 Sep 2026




                                            The PandaX Collaboration previously presented a preliminary analysis of the solar pp neutrino flux at the Neutrino
                                         2026 Conference (June 24, 2026, UC Irvine), with the slides available online [3]. A subsequent preprint detailing
                                         the full analysis was published on arXiv on July 2, 2026 (arXiv:2607.02405) [4]. This updated result represents a
                                         significant improvement over our previous measurement published in 2024 [5].
                                            While the XENONnT manuscript includes a “Note Added” acknowledging our recent preprint [4], the summary
                                         plot (Fig. 6, top panel) omits this updated data point. Instead, it displays our older, superseded 2024 measurement [5].
                                            To provide a complete and accurate record, an updated comparison is presented in Fig. 1. This plot explicitly
                                         displays the PandaX-4T (2026) result alongside the XENONnT, Borexino, and Gallium data against the Standard
                                         Solar Model (SSM) prediction.
