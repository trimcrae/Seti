                              ABUNDANCE ANALYSIS OF PRZYBYLSKrS STAR
                                                            (HD 101065)

                                                    G. Wegner and A. D. Petford

                                                         (Received 1974 April 2)


                                                               SUMMARY
                              The spectrum of Przybylski’s star (HD 101065), which is completely domi-
                              nated by lines of the rare earths, was studied using spectra in the red. From
                              this material, all observed lines were measured for wavelength and identifica-
                              tions attempted. From the resulting list of apparently unblended lines, a
                              curve of growth analysis of the star was carried out. This analysis confirms
                              Przybylski’s result that very high overabundances of the rare earths by five
                              orders of magnitude compared to cosmic abundances exist when allowance
                              is made for the difference in adopted atmospheric parameters in the two
                              studies. Also the presence of previously unreported elements of the iron-
                              peak and lighter elements is established and their abundances are found to
                              be closer to normal with respect to the Sun if the star’s hydrogen abundance
                              is assumed to be normal. From the derived value of the excitation temperature
                              from the curve of growth (Texc = 6267 ± 120 K) and Balmer line strengths,
                              the star is probably a dwarf of spectral class Fop. This seems consistent
                              with the kinematical properties of the star wrhich place it in the young disk
                              population. The abilities of some current theories to explain the abundance
                              anomalies are briefly discussed and a few suggestions for future study are
                              given.


                                                         I. INTRODUCTION

                            The peculiar spectrum of the southern star HD 101065 was first described by
                        Przybylski (1961). Basic astronomical data are given in Table I where it is seen
                        that not much can be said about the star’s kinematics other than it does not have
                        particularly large space motions, probably placing it as a member of the young disk
                        population.
                            On the basis of low dispersion spectra and broadband photometry, Przybylski
                        as well as Kron & Gordon (1961) classed the star as a dwarf with high metal
                        content and effective temperature near that of spectral class Go. Subsequent
                        investigations by Przybylski & Kennedy (1963) and Przybylski (1963) based on
                        6-7 Â mm-1 Coude spectra in the blue photographic region revealed the presence
                        of an immense number of strong lines belonging to many of the singly ionized rare
                        earth elements including holmium. The severe difficulties associated with any
                        study of this star are shown by their results, where about 3000 lines were measured
                        in the wavelength interval 3600-4950 Â. Keeping in mind that any analysis of
                        HD 101065 based on blue spectra must be strongly influenced by line blending
                        (it does not appear that the true continuum can ever be seen below 5000 Â),
                        Przybylski (1966) found that compared with the Sun, the rare earths are over-
                        abundant by three orders of magnitude. Due to the lack of energy level classifica-
                        tions for some of the rare earth spectra at the time of Przybylski’s analysis, and

                        produced by the mixing of r-process products with the stellar material.

                        stars which seem to have observable lines of heavy elements such as Th and U
                        and radioactive elements such as Pm. The review by Kuchowicz (1973) presents
                        arguments for the spontaneous fission of super-heavy elements producing the
                        peculiar abundances of these stars and suggests HD 101065 as a good candidate.
                         Hack & Struve (1970) have reviewed some of the ideas regarding the origin of the
                        Ap stars as does the work of Adelman (1973) on the cool Ap stars. Since HD 101065
                         seems to have one of the most extreme over-abundances of the rare earths yet
                         observed, it could represent an important clue to our understanding of the physical
                         processes producing these stars.

                                                       2. NEW OBSERVATIONS
                            The spectra of HD 101065 used in this investigation are listed in Table II.
                        The spectrograms were obtained in 1972 May using the second order of the ‘ C ’
                        grating and the 32-in. camera of the Coude spectrograph of the Mount Stromlo
                        74-in. telescope which gives a reciprocal dispersion of i0'2 Â mm-1 on the plate.
                        A Carnegie image-tube and baked Eastman IIa-0 plates were employed as the
                        detector. A projected slit width of 0-43 Â on the plate was used and in order to
                        reduce the grain noise of the image-tube and plate combination, the spectra were
                        widened to 1-4 mm. Because of the limited field of the image-tube which can only
                        observe about 50 mm of spectrum at a time, each spectrogram covered an interval
                        of approximately 300 Â and adjoining spectra were overlapped. Consequently, for
                        5257'52 Â < À < 6764-73 Â each region is covered by at least two spectra. Compari-
                        son of intensity tracings made from these new plates with similar tracings kindly
                        loaned to us by A. Przybylski and portions of a 13-6 Â mm-1 spectrogram published
                        by Warner (1966) showed that the resolution of our new spectra was better and the
                        grain noise was markedly reduced enabling weaker spectral lines to be detected
                        (see Fig. 3 and compare with Warner’s (1966) Fig. 1).


                                3. MEASUREMENT AND REDUCTION OF THE SPECTROGRAMS

                        3. i Wavelength measures
                            To make possible a rigorous search for weak lines due to previously unreported
                        elements in the spectrum of HD 101065 and to provide a reasonably unblended
                        line list for an atmospheric analysis, all of the lines visible on all of the spectra
                        were measured. Each plate was measured in the direct and reverse directions by
                        GW on the digitized Hilger screw measuring machine at Oxford which has a

                        punched paper tape output. All subsequent data reductions were done with the
                        Nova 1200 computer at the Department of Astrophysics in Oxford whereby plate
                        measures were converted to wavelength in the usual manner by linearly inter-
                        polating between two known comparison lines at the ends of each plate and then
                        constructing a correction curve from the comparison lines using the method of
                        least squares. The comparison spectrum was provided by an Ar I lamp and by
                        overlapping second and third order spectra. The curvature of the image-tube
                        spectra produces asymmetric correction curves, but this could be adequately
                        handled if the extreme ends of each spectrogram were not used and measurements
                        were restricted nearer to the centres of the plates where the spectra are reasonably
                        linear.
                            We have estimated the internal accuracy of the wavelength measures from the
                        distribution of standard deviations for individual measures of lines measured on
                        two plates in direct and reverse directions, and find that a probable internal error
                        of ± 0*015 Â is to be expected. Systematic errors of up to several times this value
                        may exist near the ends of some of the plates listed in Table I and for À > 6900 Â
                        where not many comparison lines were available.

                        3.2 Equivalent width measures
                            Direct intensity tracings of the spectra were produced at Mount Stromlo using
                        the Göttingen microphotometer. From these tracings, the equivalent widths for
                        most measured lines were determined using a calibration of the central depth
                        which was obtained using about 15 apparently unblended lines of different strength
                        on each plate. This method has been described in detail by Tech (1971). From the
                        adopted calibration of equivalent width against central depth it appears that an
                        error of up to ± 20 per cent is to be expected in the equivalent width of a single
                        line.

                                                    4. THE COARSE ANALYSIS
                        4. i Introduction

                            The procedure adopted for the present analysis of HD 101065 utilizes the
                        method of the curve of growth which has been described in detail, e.g. by Unsold
                        (1955) and Aller (1963). The fine analysis technique hardly seems justified at the
                        present time. More precise values of oscillator strengths and assessment of the
                        effects of line blanketing on the structure of the atmosphere in this peculiar star
                        are needed. Consequently, the fundamental problem with a spectrum analysis of
                        a star like HD 101065 stems from the great richness of the rare earth lines and the
                        fact that complete energy level classifications and accurate oscillator strengths are
                        unavailable.
                            For the case of HD 101065, there are no obvious standard stars to be used for a
                        differential curve of growth analysis and we have been forced to determine abun-
                        dances in the star’s atmosphere using absolute oscillator strengths. Details of our
                        adopted scale of log ¿/and atomic data are given below.


                        4.2 Oscillator strengths and atomic data
                            Reliable values of log gf are available for relatively few of the elements and are
                        mainly confined to the strongest lines. For the rare earths, the only available
                        source of log ¿/has been the work of Corliss & Bozman (1962) which is based on
                        the extensive spectral line intensity data of Meggers et ah (1961).
                            It is well known that any analysis of a star is highly sensitive to systematic errors
                        in the scale of oscillator strengths for different elements and the Corliss & Bozman
                        work, besides suffering from internal errors, is now known to be systematically in
                        error, often by large amounts, for many elements as a function of the upper excita-
                        tion level, xe, of the line. Thus for lines not having other reliable log ¿/values, we
                        have chosen to adopt the procedure given by Takens (1970) to re-compute log¿/
                        for lines having intensity values given by Meggers et ah (1961) which takes into
                        account the following effects not included by Corliss & Bozman:
                            (a) Non-homogeneity of the arc. A model for the arc consisting of a hot and a
                        cool region is adopted.
                            (b) Non-LTE effects due to the time required by atoms entering the arc to
                        attain their LTE populations.
                            (c) Improved persistence time for atoms in the arc based on the work of de
                        Galan (1965).
                            (d) Self absorption in the arc.
                            (e) The combination of the above effects results in a normalization function
                        C(xe) which depends on the element, its ionization, and excitation state. A
                        normalization constant log K = -18*20 ±0-20 was used for all our calculations

                                                             5. DISCUSSION
                        5. i Summary of the observed abundances
                             Fig. 4 summarizes the element abundances in HD 101065 where they are
                        compared with the cosmic abundances given by Allen (1973). From these results,
                        it is felt that the following effects are established :
                            (1) Lithium is overabundant and may contain some of the isotope Li6. This
                        latter conclusion should still be considered questionable, however, due to the rather
                        low dispersion of the spectra used.
                            (2) Only a few lines of the light elements: C, Na, Mg, Al, Si, S and K are
                        observed and their abundances are not very accurate. It should be kept in mind
                        that although for some of these elements, high abundances are indicated, these are
                        primarily based on weak, high excitation lines in the spectrum which is completely
                        dominated by the rare earths.
                            (3) Sc, Ti and V appear overabundant. This behaviour is different from that of
                        typical Ap and Am stars.
                            (4) Iron seems to have nearly a cosmic abundance. Except for Li, the iron-peak
                        and lighter elements are not as greatly enhanced in HD 101065 as are the rare
                        earths.
                           (5) The abundances of Sr, Y, Zr and Mo should be reliable and indicate over-
                        abundances by 2 to 4 in log N.

                        5.2 Comparison with available theories
                            Any explanation of a star as strange as HD 101065 undoubtedly could never
                        satisfy everybody. Most likely, no single theory can explain all of the peculiar stars



                          © Royal Astronomical Society • Provided by the NASA Astrophysics Data System
197 4MNRAS.168. .557W

                        572                         G. Wegner and A. D. Petford                       Vol. 168

                        and as well, no single star should be considered a proof or counterexample for given
                        theories. It is hoped, however, that Przybylzki’s star, with its low effective tempera-
                        ture and huge overabundances of the heavy elements will eventually provide an
                        important piece to the puzzle.
                            Although it has abnormally large overabundances of the rare earths, Przybylski’s
                        star is not the only object known with such properties. Another such star is HR 465.
                        While being hotter (Teff ^ io4 K), the star seems to have a similar abundance
                        curve (Aller 1972, and references therein). Spectral variations with a time scale of
                        about 23 yr indicate that the rare earths may be confined to patches on the star
                        which is slowly rotating. The sharpness of the lines in HD 101065 and lack of
                        obvious spectral variations since its discovery may indicate that it is a similar
                        object. The great overabundances observed in this class of stars put constraints
                        on the possible theories which explain these effects.
                            The low value of Teff would make HD 101065 one °f ^e coolest Ap stars.
                        Adelman (1973) in a study of the cool Ap stars has reviewed and discussed four
                        basic types of theories regarding the origin of their abundance anomalies: (1) Sur-
                        face nuclear reactions (Brancazio & Cameron 1967), (2) Interior nuclear reactions
                        (Fowler et al. 1965), (3) Diffusion processes (Michaud 1970), and (4) Magnetic
                        accretion (Havnes & Conti 1971). Kuchowicz (1973) has outlined some of the other
                        possibilities so the details of the theories will not be dwelt upon here. Searle &
                        Sargent (1967) have reviewed some of the empirical evidence which suggests that
                        the abundance anomalies are a surface phenomenon.
                            The magnetic accretion and diffusion theories predict overabundances of the
                        rare earths, but on the assumption that the star was originally of a solar composition,
                        in their present forms, these theories alone would seem to run into difficulties
                        explaining the large overabundances of the rare earths by five orders of magnitude:
                            (1) The time required by the Havnes & Conti (1971) magnetic-accretion
                        mechanism to reach an amplification of 105 for the rare earths, as explained in
                        their paper, would be: £ ~ 477. iob.NHIp.a2. V> where NH ~ 1024 is the number
                        of atoms per cm2 above f ~ o-i in an Fo star and aRs is the accretion radius where
                        Havnes & Conti estimate a ~ 220. Adopting standard values for the other quantities :
                        v ~ 10 km s-1, and p ~ 1 gives t ~ 1012 yr which is prohibitively long.
                            (2) A star of spectral class Fo could still have a small outer convection zone, so
                        the diffusion theory outlined by Watson (1970) and Smith (1971), which has some
                        success explaining the Am phenomenon, might apply to HD 101065. In order to
                        see if this is plausible, outer envelope models for solar composition stars near
                        spectral class Fo and the Main Sequence were computed for us by J. Papaloizou at
                        Oxford. The outer radiative layers have a mass of ~ 3 x 1022 g, while the radiative
                        ‘ element separation zone ’ between the outer H 1 and He 1 and lower He 11 con-
                        vection zones (see Smith 1971, Fig. 11) has mass ~8 x 1024 g. Thus if all of the
                        rare earths were pushed upward out of the intermediate radiative zone toward the
                        top of the atmosphere, only a maximum amplification of ~3Xio2 would be
                        possible. An amplification of the rare earths* element number abundance could
                        only be achieved if parts of the star down to at least ~ 5 x 104 km were involved
                        where the mass supported by these layers is ~3xio27g. This indicates that
                        modifications to the present diffusion theory are needed to explain HD 101065.
                        Either the diffusion process extends to even deeper layers of the star than pre-
                        viously envisioned or the structure of the star is altered, perhaps by the magnetic
                        field.

                             Some aspects of the abundance curve suggest that nuclear reaction processes
                        have been operative, although again none of the presently available theories which
                        are based on these mechanisms alone can explain all of the observed features.
                        The presence of Li and abundance peaks near Z = 40 and 65 might be explained
                        by surface reactions of the type of Brancazio & Cameron (1967) if a superposition
                        of different events was allowed. The high abundance of lead could indicate neutron
                        capture, but the identification of this element is still rather shaky. The high abun-
                        dances of the heavy rare earths could also be produced by the r-process, while the
                        apparent lack of promethium would place doubt on the existence of the spontaneous
                        fission of super-heavy elements in this star.

                        5.3 Suggestions for future work
                            Further progress in studying HD 101065 should be attempted. First, on the
                        theoretical side, a model atmosphere for the star and additional calculations
                        regarding the abundance enhancement mechanisms which can be applied to it are
                        needed. Second, on the observational side, the star should be carefully examined
                        for spectral and magnetic field variations. Although the basic facts regarding the
                        abundances and spectral class of the star now seem established, the next major
                        step in an understanding of the physical processes associated with HD 101065
                        come when a high resolution study of selected spectral lines becomes possible so
                        that an accurate fine analysis and determination of isotope abundances can be made.
                        Then it may well be possible to make decisive observational tests regarding the
                        validity of the proposed theories or combination of theories for peculiar abundances
                        in stars.
