WISE/CatWISE Constraints on Dysonian Waste-Heat Technosignatures in Nearby Galaxies

Abstract

We search for galaxy-scale (Dysonian) waste heat in the mid-infrared using WISE. Starting from the 2MASS Redshift Survey (2MRS), we cross-match to CatWISE2020 and AllWISE, apply standard MIR AGN/starburst vetoes (Stern, Assef R90, Jarrett), and treat W1 and W2 as stellar baselines and W3 and W4 as constraining bands. For each galaxy and for blackbody waste heat temperatures T=150T=150–600600 K, we convert W3/W4 photometry into conservative 3​σ3\sigma per-galaxy upper limits on the bolometric waste heat luminosity using the WISE bandpass (RSR) color correction. The resulting distributions have median caps of ∼(5\sim(5–9)×108L⊙9)\times 10^{8}\,L_{\odot} across T=150T=150–600600 K. Aggregated at the population level, the one-sided 95% upper bound on the fraction of nearby galaxies that could host waste heat above a given threshold monotonically decreases with threshold and asymptotes to ≃1/6500\simeq 1/6500 at high thresholds (set by the sample size). Sensitivity transitions from W4 at T≲200T\!\lesssim\!200 K to W3 at T≳300T\!\gtrsim\!300 K. Interpreted with the AGENT formalism, a fiducial Milky Way like stellar luminosity L⋆=3×1010​L⊙L_{\star}\!=\!3\times 10^{10}\,L_{\odot} implies typical per galaxy caps of α≡Lwh/L⋆≲1.7\alpha\!\equiv\!L_{\rm wh}/L_{\star}\!\lesssim\!1.7–2.9%2.9\% over T=150T=150–600600 K (e.g., α≲1.8%\alpha\!\lesssim\!1.8\% at T=300T{=}300 K). At T≃300T\simeq 300 K, no more than f95≃1.61×10−4f_{95}\simeq 1.61\times 10^{-4} (≃0.0161%\simeq 0.0161\%) of nearby galaxies can host KIII-scale systems reprocessing ≳21%\gtrsim 21\% of a Milky Way–like stellar luminosity into ∼300\sim 300 K waste heat.

I Introduction

A basic consequence of energy use is the disposal of low entropy energy as heat (Dyson, 1960). If galaxy scale engineering reprocesses a significant fraction of starlight or other power sources, the associated waste heat should emerge as approximately thermal emission with a characteristic temperature of a few ×102\times 10^{2} K, peaking in the mid-infrared (MIR). This motivates “Dysonian SETI” (Bradbury et al., 2011; Ćirković and Bradbury, 2006), which seeks astrophysical waste heat signatures rather than deliberate beacons. Lacki (2016) show that extremely cold (≪100\ll 100 K) galaxy scale “blackboxes” would violate microwave constraints and are therefore strongly disfavored. All-sky FIR/MIR searches began with IRAS, with early constraints on Dyson-like structures presented by Carrigan (2009). With WISE (Wright et al., 2010) and NEOWISE (Mainzer et al., 2014), Ĝ program (Wright et al., 2014b, a) set quantitative WISE-based upper limits on Kardashev Type III systems; subsequent work tightly constrained the reddest extended sources (Griffith et al., 2015).

Two developments now motivate a re–examination of galaxy–scale waste–heat constraints. First, the CatWISE2020 catalog (Marocco et al., 2021), built on WISE and the extended NEOWISE time baseline, delivers improved W1/W2 astrometry and photometry across the entire sky, enabling cleaner baselines for modeling stellar continua in galaxies. Second, lessons from the WISE/AllWISE (Cutri et al., 2013; Cutri and AllWISE Team, 2014) artifact taxonomy and the maturation of MIR AGN/starburst diagnostics (e.g., Stern et al. 2012; Assef et al. 2018; see also the widely used color wedges of Jarrett et al. 2011) allow us to construct transparent selection and masking policies that substantially reduce non–technological false positives.

In this paper we leverage CatWISE2020 (for W1/W2) together with AllWISE (for W1,W2,W3,W4) to place population constraints on galaxy–scale waste heat for a nearby, well–characterized parent sample. We adopt the 2MASS Redshift Survey (2MRS; Huchra et al. 2012) as our galaxy set and develop a fixed–configuration pipeline that:

  1. 1.

    cross–matches 2MRS to CatWISE2020 and AllWISE with an explicit best–match protocol and per–band artifact vetting;

  2. 2.

    defines two quality policies, Strict and Lenient, differing only in the treatment of W4 artifacts, and applies three independent AGN/starburst masks (Stern, Assef R90, and a Jarrett–style wedge) singly and in combination;

  3. 3.

    converts MIR magnitudes to fluxes (Vega zeropoints) and, for each galaxy and assumed radiator temperature T∈{150,200,300,400,600}T\in\{150,200,300,400,600\} K, derives a conservative per–object upper limit Lwhmax​(T)L_{\rm wh}^{\max}(T) using a “flux–cap” approach that takes the most constraining of W3 or W4;

  4. 4.

    aggregates these per–object limits into population upper bounds on the fraction of galaxies that could host waste–heat luminosity ≥Lwh,thr\geq L_{\rm wh,thr} at temperature TT, f95​(T,Lwh,thr)≃3/Nefff_{95}(T,L_{\rm wh,thr})\simeq 3/N_{\rm eff} (the 95%95\% upper limit for zero events).

Our analysis yields (i) distributions of LwhmaxL_{\rm wh}^{\max} across TT for the full sample, (ii) f95f_{95} curves versus threshold that quantify survey sensitivity at the population level, and (iii) empirical diagnostics showing which MIR band (W3 or W4) actually sets the tightest constraint as a function of TT. A comprehensive robustness check demonstrates that the main conclusions are insensitive to reasonable choices of artifact policy and mask combination.

This work therefore provides an updated, CatWISE–era assessment of how many K2/K3–like systems could be “hiding in plain sight” in the nearby Universe, under explicit assumptions documented in the text. The paper is organized as follows. Section II describes the parent sample and MIR catalogs. Section III details the selection, masking, photometric conversions, and upper–limit methodology. Section IV presents per–object upper–limit distributions, population constraints, and band–of–limitation statistics, while Section V examines robustness to analysis choices. Section VI discusses implications and limitations, and Section VII summarizes our conclusions. Throughout, we adopt Vega magnitudes for WISE photometry and quote waste–heat luminosities in L⊙L_{\odot}.

II Data

II.1 Parent Sample: 2MRS

We adopt the 2MASS Redshift Survey (2MRS) as our parent galaxy catalog (Huchra et al., 2012), which provides uniform all–sky positions and redshifts for nearby galaxies. For each source we retain the equatorial coordinates, redshift zz, and luminosity distance DLD_{L} as published by 2MRS.111All distances used in this work are the 2MRS luminosity distances; our conclusions do not depend on small variations in DLD_{L} for the redshift range considered. We use mid–infrared photometry from the WISE mission and its reactivation (Wright et al., 2010; Mainzer et al., 2014). Four bands are available: W1, W2, W3, and W4 (3.4, 4.6, 12, and 22 μ\mum). We combine two modern catalogs:

  • •

All magnitudes are Vega. We adopt the AllWISE zero–magnitude flux densities Fν,0={309.540, 171.787, 31.674, 8.363}F_{\nu,0}=\{309.540,\,171.787,\,31.674,\,8.363\} Jy for W1, W2, W3, W4, respectively (AllWISE Explanatory Supplement).222https://wise2.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/release/allwise/expsup/ For diagnostic purposes we also record per–band signal–to–noise (S/N) and reduced χ2\chi^{2} where available.

II.3 Cross–matching and Best–Candidate Resolution

We perform a multi–object cone search of AllWISE and CatWISE2020 at the 2MRS coordinates with a radius of 5​″5\arcsec.333The choice of 5​″5\arcsec accommodates modest centroid differences for bright or extended galaxies while avoiding excessive false matches; tightening to 3​″3\arcsec changes the sample negligibly after artifact vetting. Because a given 2MRS position may return multiple WISE candidates (e.g., blended or nearby sources), we select a single “best” counterpart in each catalog per 2MRS target by ranking candidates with the following tie–breakers, in order: (1) fewer artifacts (CatWISE: ab_flags==0 preferred; both catalogs: per–band cc_flags clean), (2) smaller angular separation from the input position, and (3) stronger W1 profile–fit quality (higher S/N or smaller σm\sigma_{\rm m}). This yields one AllWISE row and one CatWISE row (or none) per 2MRS galaxy. Final sample construction is handled in Section III.

III Methods

Our analysis converts WISE photometry and flags into per–galaxy waste–heat upper limits and then into population–level bounds. For transparency, each step is specified by explicit equations and a short pseudo code workflow (Appendix A). All configuration choices (artifact policy, AGN/starburst masks, temperature grid, and significance level) are fixed before inspecting the population results.

III.1 Band Choice and Artifact Policies

We treat W1/W2 as the stellar baseline and W3/W4 as the constraining bands for waste heat. Band selection follows:

  • •

    W1/W2 (baseline): prefer CatWISE magnitudes when ab_flags=0 and the per–band character of cc_flags is “0” (clean). If CatWISE fails these criteria, we fall back to AllWISE provided its cc_flags is clean in that band.

  • •

    W3/W4 (constraints): use AllWISE.

We define two artifact policies that differ only in the treatment of W4; in all cases W1, W2, and W3 must be clean in their respective bands:

STRICT

W1, W2, and W3 require cc_flags character “0” (clean); W4 must also have cc_flags “0”.

LENIENT

Identical to STRICT for W1, W2 and W3; W4 is retained even if its cc_flags character is non‑zero (retained with a flag).

III.2 AGN/Starburst Masks

We remove sources consistent with strong AGN or starburst MIR colors using three independent diagnostics applied to the WISE selection above:

  1. 1.

    Stern cut: (W​1−W​2)≥0.8(W1{-}W2)\geq 0.8 for bright W​2W2; we adopt W​2<15.05W2<15.05 as in Stern et al. (2012).

  2. 2.

    Assef R90: (W​1−W​2)≥ℛ90​(W​2)(W1{-}W2)\geq\mathcal{R}_{90}(W2), where ℛ90​(W​2)\mathcal{R}_{90}(W2) is the magnitude–dependent reliability–optimized color cut given by Assef et al. (2018). For reference, Assef et al. (2018) also provide the completeness–optimized C75 relation; we use R90 in the analysis.

  3. 3.

    Jarrett wedge: (W​2−W​3)≥1.3(W2{-}W3)\geq 1.3, (W​1−W​2)≤1.7(W1{-}W2)\leq 1.7, and (W​1−W​2)≥0.10​(W​2−W​3)+0.38(W1{-}W2)\geq 0.10\,(W2{-}W3)+0.38 and ≥0.315​(W​2−W​3)−0.222\geq 0.315\,(W2{-}W3)-0.222.

A KIII-like MIR excess moves sources toward large W​2−W​3W2{-}W3 and moderately large W​1−W​2W1{-}W2, overlapping the starburst/AGN locus. If such systems exist, they would be removed by our union mask when we require an astrophysically clean retained sample. However, our population limits are set by the number of galaxies for which our data would have been sensitive at a given threshold (NeffN_{\rm eff}). Figure 3 shows that using no mask, Stern only, Stern+Assef, or the full union yields statistically indistinguishable f95f_{95} curves: the asymptotic plateaus and mid‑threshold behavior are unchanged within our precision.

III.4 Per–Galaxy Waste–Heat Upper Limits

We assume that any waste–heat component from technological activity radiates approximately as a blackbody of temperature TT, and we use W3/W4 to set a conservative “flux–cap” on its allowed normalization. III.5 Population Bounds

For a fixed TT and threshold Lwh,thrL_{\rm wh,thr}, we define the effective sample size

Neff​(T,Lwh,thr)≡#​{galaxies with ​Lwhmax​(T)≤Lwh,thr},N_{\rm eff}(T,L_{\rm wh,thr})\;\equiv\;\#\bigl\{\,\text{galaxies with }L_{\rm wh}^{\max}(T)\leq L_{\rm wh,thr}\,\bigr\}, (7)

IV Results

IV.1 Working Sample and Masks

Starting from the 2MRS parent list (§II), cross–matching to CatWISE2020 and AllWISE with the best–candidate protocol of §II.3 yields a working set of ∼2.2×104\sim 2.2\times 10^{4} galaxies with usable MIR photometry. The Strict sample requires clean per–band artifact flags for all four bands, whereas the Lenient variant retains W4 even if flagged (§III.1). We then apply the union of three independent MIR AGN/starburst masks (Stern, Assef R90, and a Jarrett–style wedge; §III.2). The retained sources occupy the locus of normal galaxies in WISE color–color space, while masked objects cluster in the expected AGN/starburst regions (Fig. 6). After all selections, the number of galaxies with a valid per–object limit is temperature–independent and equals Nvalid=19,617N_{\rm valid}=19{,}617 for the Strict+full–mask sample (Table 1).

IV.2 Per–object Upper–limit Distributions

For each galaxy and each assumed radiator temperature T∈{150,200,300,400,600}​KT\in\{150,200,300,400,600\}\,\mathrm{K}, we compute a conservative upper limit Lwhmax​(T)L_{\rm wh}^{\max}(T) using the W3/W4 “flux-cap” method of §III.4, which includes bandpass integrated (RSR) color corrections and a σ\sigma–consistent handling of non detections. These benchmarks enable direct comparison to prior WISE based technosignature searches.

IV.4 Which Band Limits the Upper Limit?

Because Lwhmax​(T)L_{\rm wh}^{\max}(T) is the minimum of the W3– and W4–based caps (§III.4), it is informative to record which band is limiting. Figure 5 shows the counts of objects limited by W3 or by W4 as a function of TT. At the coolest temperatures (150–200 K), W4 (22 μ\mum) dominates the constraints, reflecting its proximity to the thermal peak and its leverage on cool dust–like SEDs. For T≥300T\geq 300 K the balance flips: W3 (12 μ\mum) overwhelmingly sets the limit (the fraction W3–limited exceeds ∼\sim90%), consistent with W3 moving onto the spectral peak while W4 samples the Rayleigh–Jeans tail. This diagnostic explains the shapes of the f95f_{95} curves and guides future observing strategies (e.g., deeper W4 for cool radiators; improved W3 calibration/depth for warm radiators).

V Robustness and Systematics

V.1 Artifact Policy and Mask Choices

Figure 3 overlays f95​(Lwh,thr)f_{95}(L_{\rm wh,thr}) at T=300T=300 K for a policy/mask combinations. The Lenient policy (W4 relaxed) produces curves that are visually indistinguishable from Strict, as expected because (i) for T≥300T\!\geq\!300 K the limiting band is overwhelmingly W3 (Fig. 5), and (ii) relaxing W4 only affects a minority of otherwise clean sources. Mask choices (none; Stern only; Stern+Assef; full union) likewise induce negligible changes: removing masks redistributes a small number of objects but does not alter the global sensitivity. Across all combinations, the asymptotic plateau remains fixed at f95≈3/Nvalid≃1.53×10−4f_{95}\!\approx\!3/N_{\rm valid}\simeq 1.53\times 10^{-4} and the main conclusions of §IV.3 are unaffected. As an extreme bound, if all galaxies flagged by the AGN/starburst cuts were in fact KIII systems, our population constraints would revert to the “no‑mask” curves in Fig. 3; those are statistically indistinguishable from the baseline within our precision, so the limits would hardly change.

For the Strict+full–mask sample the median ratio Lwh,RSRmax/Lwh,MONOmaxL_{\rm wh,RSR}^{\max}/L_{\rm wh,MONO}^{\max} is ≃\simeq +0.8% at 150150 K, +0.8% at 200200 K (with a long high‑side tail to ∼\sim+17% among W3‑limited faint objects), +16.6% at 300300 K, +9.1% at 400400 K, and −1.7%-1.7\% at 600600 K. V.3 Non-detections and a Common k​σk\sigma Convention

Catalog non–detections (ph_qual=U) are mapped to the same significance as detections via Eq. 3, with αU≡k/kU\alpha_{U}\!\equiv\!k/k_{U} and k=3k\!=\!3, kU≃2k_{U}\!\simeq\!2. Varying kUk_{U} over [1.5,3][1.5,3] modifies only the faint‑end caps; the f95f_{95} curves at bright thresholds and their convergence are insensitive because f95∝1/Nefff_{95}\!\propto\!1/N_{\rm eff} and NeffN_{\rm eff} is dominated by objects with firm detections or strong limits.

V.4 Stellar RJ Subtraction

We set Fν,bRJ=0F^{\rm RJ}_{\nu,b}=0 in Eq. 3, i.e., we do not subtract any extrapolated stellar continuum at W3/W4. This choice is conservative, because any plausible stellar contribution would reduce Fν,bcapF^{\rm cap}_{\nu,b} and thus tighten the caps. As a robustness check we repeat the full analysis with a Rayleigh–Jeans extrapolation from W1/W2, parameterized as:

so that Fν,bRJ∝νb2F^{\rm RJ}_{\nu,b}\propto\nu_{b}^{2} (equivalently ∝λb−2\propto\lambda_{b}^{-2}). V.5 Extended–source Photometry and Systematic Floors

AllWISE profile‑fit photometry can underestimate W3/W4 fluxes for large nearby galaxies. In an upper‑limit analysis this bias makes LwhmaxL_{\rm wh}^{\max} artificially small (i.e., overly tight, not conservative). Two features mitigate any residual impact: (i) the artifact policy removes poor fits and obvious artifacts; and (ii) for T≥300T\!\geq\!300 K, W3 sets the limit for the vast majority of objects (Fig. 5), where extended‑source bias is less severe. We therefore expect any remaining bias to be subdominant relative to the bandpass and mask choices.

V.6 Synthetic injection recovery validation

We validate the end–to–end measurement model, including RSR bandpasses, noise, treatment of non–detections, and the min⁡(W3,W4)\min(\mathrm{W3},\mathrm{W4}) limiting–band rule, with a Monte Carlo injection recovery test. Each trial draws a radiator temperature TT, a luminosity distance DLD_{L} sampled from the observed 2MRS distribution, and an injected waste–heat luminosity LwhL_{\rm wh}. We then add a blackbody component of bolometric luminosity LwhL_{\rm wh} and temperature TT to a simple host mid–IR continuum.

The host continua (labeled E/Sbc/SB) are parametric toy SEDs defined in flux density FνF_{\nu} at the WISE pivot frequencies to bracket dust poor through dust rich MIR host levels relevant for a W3/W4 based cap estimator. We write the host continuum as a Rayleigh Jeans stellar tail plus a featureless warm dust plateau,

Fν,host​(νb)=A​[(νbνW1)2+fdust],F_{\nu,\rm host}(\nu_{b})=A\left[\left(\frac{\nu_{b}}{\nu_{\rm W1}}\right)^{2}+f_{\rm dust}\right], (9)

with fdust=0f_{\rm dust}=0 (E; dust–poor), fdust=0.3f_{\rm dust}=0.3 (Sbc; intermediate), and fdust=1.0f_{\rm dust}=1.0 (SB; starburst–like). The normalization AA is chosen so that the resulting host W3/W4 fluxes lie in a realistic regime for the catalog uncertainties, ensuring that the detection/non–detection logic and the k​σk\sigma cap floor are exercised under conditions comparable to the data. These continua are intended as stress tests for the recovery pipeline, not as detailed MIR spectral models.

For each synthetic galaxy we integrate the combined host+blackbody spectrum through the W3 and W4 relative system responses (RSRs), add Gaussian noise comparable to the catalog errors, apply the same detection threshold at kUk_{U} and the same k​σk\sigma cap construction as in the main analysis, and convert each band cap to a bolometric waste–heat limit. The recovered upper limit is then taken as Lwhmax=min⁡(W3,W4)L_{\rm wh}^{\max}=\min(\mathrm{W3},\mathrm{W4}), exactly matching the real–data workflow.

Figure 7 shows recovered versus injected LwhL_{\rm wh} at T=300​KT=300\,\mathrm{K}. At high injected luminosity, the recovered values follow the 1:1 relation, demonstrating unbiased recovery when the waste–heat signal dominates the host continuum and noise. At low injected luminosity, the recovered values lie above the 1:1 line because non–negativity, the k​σk\sigma floor, and the host MIR continuum yield conservative caps, as intended. Across host families, the dust–poor E case yields the tightest recoveries, Sbc is modestly looser, and SB is loosest at low LwhL_{\rm wh} due to its stronger MIR continuum; however, such SB–like systems are preferentially removed by the union mask and therefore do not drive our reported population constraints.

VI Discussion

VI.1 What the Population Bounds Mean

Our primary result is a set of temperature–dependent population bounds, f95​(T,Lwh,thr)f_{95}(T,L_{\rm wh,thr}), that quantify what fraction of nearby galaxies could host waste–heat luminosity above a threshold Lwh,thrL_{\rm wh,thr} while remaining consistent with the observed WISE W3/W4 photometry under our k​σk\sigma flux–cap model. Because f95∝1/Nefff_{95}\!\propto\!1/N_{\rm eff} at bright thresholds, the high–threshold plateaus reflect only the effective sample size: with Nvalid=19,617N_{\rm valid}=19{,}617 (Table 1), all five curves converge to f95≃3/Nvalid≃1.53×10−4f_{95}\simeq 3/N_{\rm valid}\simeq 1.53\times 10^{-4}. At lower thresholds the curves separate in the physically expected order (Fig. 2), encapsulating the different leverage of W3 and W4 on cool vs. warm blackbodies.

We intentionally refrain from imposing host–galaxy priors when presenting limits. For context, however, the single–object medians in Table 1 span p50≃(5.0p50\simeq(5.0–8.6)×108L⊙8.6)\times 10^{8}\,L_{\odot} from T=150T=150–600600 K. If one adopts a representative stellar luminosity L⋆∼L_{\star}\sim few ×1010​L⊙\times 10^{10}\,L_{\odot}, these caps correspond to order–percent fractional waste heat per galaxy; the exact mapping is population–dependent and not required for our conclusions. \startlongtable

VI.2 Astrophysical Contaminants and the Transparency Table

Our masking removes the most MIR–AGN–like and starburst–like colors (Stern, Assef R90, Jarrett; Fig. 6), but the retained set still contains red systems with enhanced W​2−W​3W2{-}W3 that are astrophysically mundane (e.g., dusty starbursts outside the strict wedge). The T=300T=300 K “outliers” (top-50 loosest caps) are provided as a machine-readable ancillary table with this submission, outliers_T300_top50.csv. A key limitation of MIR-only Dyson-sphere searches is the degeneracy between warm waste heat and ordinary interstellar dust emission. Dust-rich, star-forming galaxies can exhibit strong W3/W4 continua that are spectrally similar to a few×102\times 10^{2} K thermal component, inflating the flux caps and therefore yielding systematically looser LwhmaxL_{\rm wh}^{\max} at T∼200T\sim 200–400400 K even in the absence of any technosignature. This is the dominant astrophysical confounder behind the high-LwhmaxL_{\rm wh}^{\max} tail and is why dust-poor (elliptical-like) systems provide markedly tighter constraints (VI.5).

VI.3 Systematics: What Matters and What Does Not

Three potential systematics were isolated and quantified in §V. First, the bandpass treatment is material in the mid–threshold regime: using the WISE RSR color correction increases individual LwhmaxL_{\rm wh}^{\max} by ∼0\sim 0–1% (150–200 K), ∼16%\sim 16\% (300 K), ∼9%\sim 9\% (400 K), and −1.7%-1.7\% (600 K) relative to a monochromatic mapping, as predicted by ⟨Bν⟩/Bν​(νpiv)\langle B_{\nu}\rangle/B_{\nu}(\nu_{\rm piv}) and verified. Second, mapping catalog non–detections to the same k​σk\sigma as detections (Eq. 3) primarily affects the faint end and leaves NeffN_{\rm eff}–dominated plateaus unchanged. Residual effects are subdominant because (i) the artifact policy removes poor photometry and (ii) W3 sets most limits for T≥300T\!\geq\!300 K (Fig. 5). Additional checks—RJ subtraction, policy/mask variants—produce only modest, shape–preserving changes to the curves (Fig. 3).

VI.4 Dependence on Radiator Temperature and a Path to Improvement

The limiting–band statistics (Fig. 5) provide a practical roadmap. For cool radiators (T≤200T\!\leq\!200 K), W4 (22 μ\mum) controls sensitivity; deeper W4 imaging, improved large–scale background modeling, or better artifact rejection would directly tighten the cool–TT curves. For warm radiators (T≥300T\!\geq\!300 K), W3 (12 μ\mum) dominates; improved W3 calibration and depth would yield the greatest leverage. Multi–wavelength anchoring of the long–wavelength SED (e.g., with far–IR photometry) would further reduce dependence on MIR color selection and sharpen the interpretation of red outliers.

VI.5 Empirical null tests across galaxy types

We split the Strict+union sample by MIR color as a proxy for morphology (“elliptical‑like” with W​2−W​3<1.0W2{-}W3<1.0 and “spiral‑like” with W​2−W​3≥1.0W2{-}W3\geq 1.0), excluding the Jarrett wedge, and recompute f95f_{95} at T=300T=300 K. All elliptical‑like galaxies (N=5,827N=5{,}827) have Lwhmax≤109​L⊙L_{\rm wh}^{\max}\!\leq 10^{9}\,L_{\odot}, so the curve sits on its plateau 3/N=5.15×10−43/N=5.15\times 10^{-4} across the plotted range, whereas spiral‑like galaxies (N=13,790N=13{,}790) behave like the full sample, approaching 3/N=2.18×10−43/N=2.18\times 10^{-4} at large thresholds (Supplementary Fig. 8). This supports a “null” (no‑KIII) universe in which ordinary MIR color variations are driven by ISM rather than waste heat.

VI.6 Mapping to AGENT α\alpha

In the AGENT formalism (Wright et al., 2014b), the parameter α\alpha is the fraction of starlight reprocessed into waste heat, so α≈Lwh/L⋆\alpha\approx L_{\rm wh}/L_{\star} for starlight-powered systems. Our per-object limits LwhmaxL_{\rm wh}^{\max} therefore imply αmax≈Lwhmax/L⋆\alpha^{\max}\approx L_{\rm wh}^{\max}/L_{\star}. Because we did not model L⋆L_{\star} per galaxy in this work, we present a fiducial translation using L⋆=3×1010​ℒ⊙L_{\star}=3\times 10^{10}\,\mathcal{L}_{\odot} (Milky Way-like): the median caps in Table 1 correspond to αmedianmax≈1.7%\alpha^{\max}_{\rm median}\approx 1.7\% (150–300 K), 1.9%1.9\% (400 K), and 2.9%2.9\% (600 K). A future extension using, e.g., 2MRS KsK_{s} photometry to estimate L⋆L_{\star} per galaxy would yield a distribution of αmax\alpha^{\max} values; none of our population conclusions rely on this additional modeling.

VII Conclusions

We presented a WISE based search for Dysonian waste heat in nearby galaxies. The analysis (i) implements bandpass integrated relative spectral response (RSR) color corrections for W3/W4, (ii) treats non detections with a σ\sigma–consistent flux cap, and (iii) prefers profile–fit photometry, with extended source photometry assessed as a systematic. Applied to a ∼2.2×104\sim 2.2\times 10^{4}–object working sample, the Strict+full–mask subset yields Nvalid=19,617N_{\mathrm{valid}}=19{,}617 per object limits across T∈{150,200,300,400,600}​KT\in\{150,200,300,400,600\}\,\mathrm{K}.

Our main findings are:

  1. 1.

    The population bounds f95​(T,Lwh,thr)f_{95}(T,L_{\rm wh,thr}) are monotonic in threshold and converge at large Lwh,thrL_{\rm wh,thr} to the binomial plateau f95≈3/Nvalid≃1.53×10−4f_{95}\!\approx\!3/N_{\rm valid}\simeq 1.53\times 10^{-4} (Figs. 2, 4).

  2. 2.

    The limiting band transitions from W4 at T≤200T\!\leq\!200 K to W3 at T≥300T\!\geq\!300 K (Fig. 5), explaining the families of curves and highlighting where deeper data would most improve sensitivity.

  3. 3.

    Proper W3/W4 bandpass treatment matters at mid thresholds: relative to a monochromatic mapping, the RSR correction raises individual caps by ∼\sim16% at 300 K (and ∼\sim9% at 400 K), while leaving the high–threshold plateau fixed by NeffN_{\rm eff}.

  4. 4.

    Mask choices, artifact policy (STRICT vs. LENIENT), and RJ subtraction induce only modest, shape–preserving changes; the conclusions are robust (Fig. 3).

At the population level, the one-sided 95% upper limit reaches ≃1/6500\simeq 1/6500 (i.e., f95≈1.53×10−4f_{95}\!\approx\!1.53\times 10^{-4}) at high thresholds, set purely by sample size. Interpreted via AGENT, typical per-galaxy caps are α≲(1.7\alpha\lesssim(1.7–2.9)%2.9)\% for T=150T{=}150–600600 K when scaled to a Milky Way-like L⋆L_{\star}.

Appendix A Workflow pseudo-code

INPUT: 2MRS positions/redshifts, AllWISE Source Catalog, CatWISE2020
FOR each 2MRS galaxy:
  - Cone-search AllWISE and CatWISE2020 within 5"
  - Select one "best" counterpart per catalog by:
      (1) cleanest artifact flags; (2) smallest separation; (3) W1 profile-fit quality
  - Adopt W1/W2 (baseline): prefer CatWISE if clean, else AllWISE if clean
  - Adopt W3/W4 (constraints): AllWISE (retain W4 based on policy)
Apply artifact policy:
  STRICT: W1 TO W4 ALL clean
  LENIENT: W1 TO W3 clean AND W4 kept even if flagged (marked)
Compute colors; apply AGN/starburst masks:
  - Stern: (W1-W2) >= 0.8 if W2 < 15.05
  - Assef R90: (W1-W2) >= R90(W2)
  - Jarrett wedge: domain + two oblique boundaries
Select retained sample (union mask if desired)
FOR each T in {150,200,300,400,600} K:
  FOR each constraining band b in {W3,W4}:
    - Form 3-sigma cap F_nu,b^cap using detections/non-detections consistently
    - Convert to bolometric cap with WISE RSR: F_bol^(b)(T)
    - L_wh^(b)(T) = 4*pi D_L^2 F_bol^(b)(T)
  - L_wh^max(T) = min[L_wh^(W3)(T), L_wh^(W4)(T)]
Aggregate:
  - Distributions of L_wh^max(T) (p50, p90, ...)
  - N_eff(T, L_thr) = count[L_wh^max(T) <= L_thr]
  - f_95(T, L_thr) ~= 3 / N_eff(T, L_thr)  (zero-event upper bound)
ROBUSTNESS:
  - Policy variants (STRICT/LENIENT), masks (none/Stern/Stern+Assef/union)
  - RSR vs monochromatic at pivot, RJ subtraction ON/OFF
  - Extended-source stress (+10% on limiting band)
