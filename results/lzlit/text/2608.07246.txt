                                                  Abstract. The quantum-mechanical two-sided Bogoliubov inequality provides upper and
                                                  lower bounds for the free energy required to separate a system of interacting particles into
                                                  independent subsystems. The bounds can be calculated straightforwardly from the ensemble
                                                  average of the interface energy, bypassing the direct evaluation of the free energy. In this work,
                                                  we generalize the two-sided Bogoliubov inequality to arbitrary von Neumann algebras by
                                                  employing the Araki-Uhlmann relative entropy and the framework of unbounded perturbation
                                                  theory of KMS states. Furthermore, we obtain variational expressions for the relative free

                                                  Crucially, these mathematical developments yield a physically well-founded thermodynamic

                                                                                       1. Introduction

                                          1.1. Quantifying correlations. In quantum systems consisting of infinitely many degrees
                                          of freedom, states across two subsystems can be infinitely entangled, and the theory of von
                                          Neumann algebras provides a powerful mathematical framework in which these correlations
                                          can be formalized [34, 67, 71]. Natural realizations of such systems are given by quantum
                                          field theoretic models of interacting particles, for example, quantum fields of spins serving as
                                          realistic prototypes for modern quantum simulators. The future of quantum computation, and
                                          more generally of quantum technology, requires a detailed understanding of these systems, in
                                          particular, the ability of practitioners to quantify entanglement and transfer this information
                                          onto optimally designed devices [1, 35].
                                             Common entanglement measures, which quantify the difference between a reference state and
                                          the separable states of the system, are the relative entropy of entanglement, the entanglement
                                          cost, and the distillable entanglement, to name a few [33, 51, 69, 70]; they are amenable to
                                          calculations or estimations using state-of-the-art computational protocols [28, 36]. In recent
                                          years, considerable progress has been made in the mathematical analysis of the role and
                                          properties of relative entropy-like quantities [32, 59, 68]. While the perspective of physical
                                          applications is rather promising, complementary paths that avoid direct calculations of the
                                          relative entropy, if physically well founded and mathematically rigorous, can lead to other

   We propose a free energy-like criterion to quantify quantum entanglement by establishing a
generalization of the quantum-mechanical two-sided Bogoliubov inequality [20, 55] within the
mathematical framework of von Neumann algebras; compared to the previously studied case,
this generalized inequality offers an analytic tool for an extended range of physical situations,
namely, systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom. The inequality provides upper
and lower bounds for the free energy cost required for separating a system of interacting
particles into two or more non-interacting subsystems. For the prototypical bipartite quantum
systems, this leads to a free energy estimate of the correlations between particles located in
different subsystems, which can hence be measured thermodynamically; in this context, it
should be noted that formal correspondences between thermodynamics and entanglement
have been known for quite some time [52]. In the past, the two-sided Bogoliubov inequality
has been applied to low-temperature dense quantum gases with finite-range interactions [21]
and to a gas of interacting electrons [57], and it has been used to quantify finite-size effects in
molecular simulations [56, 58]. The connection between electron correlations and quantum
entanglement [19] justifies the idea that the inequality may also be employed to quantify
the entanglement between separated subsystems, with the free energy of separation serving
as a direct thermodynamic estimate of it. Indeed, the difference between the free energy of
the entire system and of the system divided in two identical but separated, non-interacting
parts expresses the difference in correlations across the two parts. Moreover, from the point
of view of experimental physics, where entanglement is usually measured through highly
complex techniques involving delicate preparation and careful manipulation of quantum states
[38, 72], thermodynamic (calorimetric) measurements complementing the standard techniques
are already being employed in the design of modern quantum materials [15, 39]; while these
measurements are not yet used to detect quantum entanglement directly, a possible path for
achieving this has been proposed very recently [9, 61].
   Connected with the quantum-mechanical two-sided Bogoliubov inequality, there are also
variational bounds for the free energy of separation [55]; they will be generalized to the setting
of von Neumann algebras as well. This makes it possible, in principle, to optimize the bounds
with respect to some key physical quantities of the system, namely, states and perturbations.
In applications to materials modeling, an optimization procedure of this kind could eventually
lead to an optimal design of a physical system with prescribed quantum characteristics.
   In conclusion, in this paper we provide the conceptual justification, with its proper mathe-
matical structure, for the possibility of measuring entanglement through thermodynamics,
while we leave concrete applications of this criterion to systems of interest for future work.

Bogoliubov inequality in Section 2. Its desired extension to general von Neumann algebras
essentially rests on two mathematical pillars: the Araki-Uhlmann relative entropy [6, 7, 65] and
the framework of unbounded perturbation theory of KMS states developed by J. Dereziński,
V. Jakšić, and C.-A. Pillet [22]. Consequently, to set the stage for the extension, we will
recall the necessary background from modular theory in Section 3 and present the key ideas
of W ∗ -dynamical perturbation theory, the results of Ref. [22], as well as some additions to
these results in Section 4.
   Our first main result, the two-sided Bogoliubov inequality in von Neumann algebras, will
be established in Section 5. Leaving aside some details, in its essential form it can be stated
as follows:

Theorem (Informal; see Theorem 5.4). Let M ⊆ B(H) be a von Neumann algebra on a
Hilbert space H, let ω be a faithful KMS-state on M, let U be a self-adjoint operator affiliated

                                    ωU (U ) ≤ F (ωU , ω) ≤ ω(U ) .                              (1)

   F(ωU , ω) denotes the relative free energy between the perturbed and unperturbed state,

mechanics of finitely many degrees of freedom, then Eq. (1) reduces to the quantum-mechanical
two-sided Bogoliubov inequality (cf. Proposition 5.7).

       3. von Neumann algebras, modular theory, and relative entropy

   To pave the way for the extension of the two-sided Bogoliubov inequality, we will now briefly
recall some background material from the theory of von Neumann algebras, in particular,
concerning modular theory and the relative entropy functional; all results stated in this section
are well-known in the literature.


3.1. Some fundamentals. Let H be a Hilbert space. A von Neumann algebra is a strongly
(equivalently, weakly) closed ∗-subalgebra M ⊆ B(H) of bounded linear operators on H
containing the identity IdH . Since B(H) is isomorphic to the dual space of (B1 (H), ∥ · ∥tr ),
one can also consider the weak-∗ topology σ(B(H), B1 (H)) on M, referred to as the σ-weak
topology, and show that every von Neumann algebra is σ-weakly closed.
   Let M∗ ⊆ M∗ denote the set of all σ-weakly continuous linear functionals φ : M → C. A
positive element φ ∈ M∗ , that is, a functional satisfying φ(A∗ A) ≥ 0 for all A ∈ M, is called
a normal functional, and the set of these is denoted by the symbol M+ ∗ ; a normal functional φ
with φ(IdH ) = 1 is called a normal state, and their set is denoted by Σ∗ (M). Given a vector
ξ ∈ H, the functional ωξ : M → C defined by

                                  ωξ (A) := ⟨ξ, Aξ⟩ ,   A ∈ M,

is called vector functional induced by ξ, and ξ is called its vector representative; it holds that
ωξ ∈ M+                                                            +
         ∗ and ωξ ∈ Σ∗ (M) iff ∥ξ∥ = 1. The support of φ ∈ M∗ , denoted by s(φ) or sφ , is
defined to be the smallest projection in M satisfying

                                        φ(sφ ) = φ(IdH ) .

Let M′ denote the commutant of M, that is, the set of all B ∈ B(H) which commute with
every A ∈ M. Then the support sξ := s(ωξ ) of the vector functional ωξ = ⟨ξ, · ξ⟩, ξ ∈ H, is
determined as follows [63, Sect. 5.22]:

                                        ran(sξ ) = M′ ξ .

   A closed, densely defined linear operator T : H ⊇ dom(T ) → H is said to be affiliated with
the von Neumann algebra M ⊆ B(H), and the set of these operators is denoted by M(η) , if
for all A′ ∈ M′ there holds

                    A′ dom(T ) ⊆ dom(T )     and    A′ T = T A′ on dom(T ) .

If T is self-adjoint, this is to say that T ∈ M(η) if and only if T strongly commutes with all
elements of the commutant M′ . Moreover, if T ∈ M(η) is self-adjoint and f : R → C is a
bounded Borel-measurable function, it holds that f (T ) ∈ M(η) [63, p. 226].

3.3. Standard form representation. For the discussion of the relative modular operator
as well as the development of perturbation theory, the standard form representation of von
Neumann algebras, developed independently by H. Araki [5], A. Connes [16], and U. Haagerup
[30], is an indispensable tool.
   A von Neumann algebra in standard form is a quadruple (M, H, J, P) consisting of a von
Neumann algebra M represented faithfully on H, an anti-linear unitary involution J : H → H,
and a closed self-dual cone P ⊆ H (called natural positive cone) satisfying

    (a) JMJ = M′ ;
    (b) JAJ = A∗ for all A ∈ M ∩ M′ ;
    (c) Jξ = ξ for all ξ ∈ P;
    (d) AJAJP ⊆ P for all A ∈ M.

  For a σ-finite M with cyclic and separating vector Ω ∈ H, one can take J to be the modular
conjugation JΩ from Section 3.2 and define P := {AJAΩ : A ∈ M}; thus, every σ-finite von
Neumann algebra possesses a standard form representation [31, Thm. 3.2]. More generally,
Haagerup [30] showed that every von Neumann algebra possesses a standard form and that
the quadruple (M, H, J, P) is unique up to unitary equivalence [63, Sect. 10.26].
  The following simple fact [31, Lem. 3.9] is used often below.

Lemma 3.2. Let (M, H, J, P) be a von Neumann algebra in standard form and Ω ∈ P. Then
Ω is cyclic for M if and only if Ω is separating for M.

                  4. Perturbation theory in von Neumann algebras

   In the quantum-mechanical two-sided Bogoliubov inequality discussed in Section 2, key
roles were played by the perturbation U of the “free” Hamiltonian H0 as well as the Gibbs
states ρ0 and ρ corresponding to H0 and H = H0 + U . In arbitrary von Neumann algebras,
Hamiltonians and, a fortiori, Gibbs states do not exist in general. Therefore, we first have to
introduce a suitable framework with which the two-sided Bogoliubov can be extended to this
setting. The fundamental notion turns out to be that of a W ∗ -dynamics on a von Neumann
algebra, extending the well-known concept of time evolution in quantum mechanics (which
did not play a role in Section 2 due to the availability of Hamiltonians). This concept allows
for the definition of Liouvillians, replacing the Hamiltonian from quantum mechanics, KMS
states, serving as a generalization of Gibbs states, and finally perturbations of Liouvillians
and KMS states, compensating for the decomposition H = H0 + U .
   In the following, we will first define these concepts in Section 4.1, then recall the foundations
of bounded perturbation theory in Section 4.2, after that review some more recent results
pertaining to unbounded perturbations in Section 4.3, and finally prove some more auxiliary
lemmas in Section 4.4 that will be crucial for us in the next section.

5.2. Two-sided Bogoliubov inequality. The last thing that is missing in order to generalize
Eq. (6) to arbitrary von Neumann algebras is to define an analog of the relative free energy

Definition 5.2. Let (M, H, J, P) be a von Neumann algebra in standard form, let τ be a

Remark 5.5. As in the quantum-mechanical case (cf. Remark 2.2), the upper bound
−β −1 log(∥Ω−βU ∥2 ) ≤ ω(U ) is a version of the Peierls-Bogoliubov inequality. In von Neumann

its typical form, this inequality reads

The lower bound ωU (U ) ≤ −β −1 log(∥Ω−βU ∥2 ), on the other hand, does not seem to have

                                 6. Conclusions and outlook

   We have generalized the two-sided Bogoliubov inequality [55] to the setting of von Neumann
algebras, utilizing the framework of unbounded perturbation theory of KMS states from
Ref. [22] as well as some new extensions of it. Furthermore, we have also generalized the
variational bounds for the relative free energy [55] by extending the known variational principles
for the relative entropy in terms of bounded perturbations [49] to the unbounded case. The
extension of the results of Ref. [55] to general von Neumann algebras has been motivated
by the idea to establish a thermodynamic criterion for the quantification of entanglement in
terms of the free energy of separation, which is applicable not only to finite quantum systems
but also to systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom, like quantum fields of spins, as
they play an increasingly important role in the development of quantum technology.
   While the current contribution was devoted to establishing the criterion on a mathematically
solid basis, in future work we intend to apply the inequality to concrete systems in order to
illustrate its utility. On the mathematical side, it would also be interesting to investigate
the question whether the domains of the infimum and supremum in Theorem 5.15 can be
enlarged, that is, whether the essential self-adjointness condition related to the relative
modular operator can be replaced or dropped. Finally, on a more foundational level, we
note that there still remain some conceptual difficulties about the relativistic covariance
of thermodynamic properties like the KMS condition or thermalization; see Ref. [45] for
a detailed discussion of this topic. Therefore, the physical interpretation of the quantities
entering the two-sided Bogoliubov inequality needs to be handled with some care in the
context of relativistic quantum field theories.

                         Appendix B. Strong resolvent convergence

  Let (Tn )n∈N and T be self-adjoint linear operators acting in a Hilbert space H. The
sequence (Tn )n∈N is said to converge to the operator T in the strong resolvent sense iff
            THE TWO-SIDED BOGOLIUBOV INEQUALITY IN VON NEUMANN ALGEBRAS                        35

(Tn − i)−1 → (T − i)−1 in the strong operator topology, that is,

                       ∀ ξ ∈ H : lim (Tn − i)−1 ξ − (T − i)−1 ξ = 0 .
                                   n→∞

In the following, we collect some propositions characterizing strong resolvent convergence
from the literature, which are needed in the main part of the paper.
   The first result, which is proved, e.g., in Refs. [18, Prop. 10.1.18] and [53, Thm. VIII.25],
shows that strong convergence on a core implies strong resolvent convergence.

Proposition B.1. Let T and (Tn )n∈N be self-adjoint operators acting in H. Suppose that
D ⊆ H is a subspace contained in dom(T ) and dom(Tn ) for all n ∈ N, and that T is essentially
self-adjoint on D. Furthermore, assume that limn→∞ Tn ξ = T ξ for all ξ ∈ D. Then Tn → T
in the strong resolvent sense.

  The next result, taken from and proved in Ref. [22, Prop. A.4], is used to construct a
weakly convergent sequence of vectors from operators converging in the strong resolvent sense.

Proposition B.2. Let T and (Tn )n∈N be self-adjoint operators acting in H such that Tn → T
in the strong resolvent sense. Furthermore, suppose that (Ωn )n∈N ⊆ H and Ω ∈ H are vectors
such that Ωn → Ω weakly, and that there exists a constant C ≥ 0 such that for all n ∈ N,
∥Tn Ωn ∥ ≤ C. Then Ω ∈ dom(T ), w-limn→∞ Tn Ωn exists, and T Ω = w-limn→∞ Tn Ωn .

  The following well-known result characterizes strong resolvent convergence in terms of strong
convergence of bounded functions of the operators [18, Prop. 10.1.9], [53, Thm. VIII.20].

Proposition B.3. Tn → T in the strong resolvent sense if and only if f (Tn ) → f (T ) in the
strong operator topology for all bounded continuous f : R → C.

  The final result is an immediate consequence of the previous one.

Proposition B.4. Let (Tn )n∈N be a sequence of self-adjoint operators acting in a Hilbert
space H and converging in the strong resolvent sense to a self-adjoint operator T . Then for
any continuous real-valued function g : R → R, it holds that g(Tn ) → g(T ) in the strong
resolvent sense as n → +∞.
