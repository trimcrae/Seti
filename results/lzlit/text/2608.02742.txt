                                                                                           I.   INTRODUCTION

                                           Ultralight fields with masses in the range of m ∼ 10−19 eV and a Compton-wavelength of a few kpc can be viable
                                        candidates for the “fuzzy dark matter” [2, 3] which can solve some of the problems of the standard cold-dark matter,
                                        like the core-cusp problem and missing satellite problem [4]. It is an interesting coincidence that the time-period of
                                        compact-binary pulsars like the Hulse-Taylor binary [5] T ∼ 8 hrs corresponds to the angular frequency ω ∼ 2π/P ∼
                                        10−19 eV which is in the range of fuzzy-dark matter mass. Monitoring the dynamics of the Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar
                                        and other compact binary systems was the first indirect test of the existence of gravitational waves [6]. The time-
                                        period of the binary orbits decreases at the rate Ṗ ∼ 10−12 s/s, which agrees with the prediction from the calculation
                                        of period loss expected from binaries in elliptical orbits due to gravitational waves by Peters and Mathews [7]. Hulse-
                                        Taylor type binaries can also radiate scalar and vector particles as long as their masses are smaller than the orbital
                                        frequency. Therefore, compact binary dynamics is a good probe of ultralight scalar [8–10] or vector [11] radiation,
                                        as there will be additional channels of energy loss of binary orbits due to radiation of scalar and vector particles in
                                        addition to gravitational waves. The decrease in the total energy of the binary due to the radiation of gravitational
                                        waves and other ultralight particles reduces the relative distance and the orbital period, as observed experimentally.
                                        This observation is then used to constrain the couplings and masses of the possible extra long-range fields associated
                                        with the neutron star, white dwarf, or black holes in the binary. For binaries in elliptical orbits, there is a loss of the
                                        binary’s total angular momentum, resulting in a decrease in orbital eccentricity, which can be observed. The total
                                        angular momentum carried away by gravitational waves from binaries was calculated by Peters [12].
                                           Recently, Page [1] used the Peters-Mathews formula for energy radiated [7] and the Peters formula [12] for total
                                        angular momentum radiated due to gravitational waves, and pointed out a curious fact that the ratio of the angular
                                        momentum radiated in gravitational radiation with the number of gravitons, i.e., J/   ˙ Ṅ is approximately equal to 2ℏ.
                                        Here, the graviton number is apsecondary quantity derived from the primary observable, the rate of energy radiated
                                        as Ṅ ≡ Ė/(ℏω0 ), where ω0 = GM/a3 is the fundamental angular frequency of the elliptical orbit.
                                           In this paper, we examine the angular momentum carried away by scalar, vector, and graviton fields. We compute
                                        the total angular momentum and energy carried away by fields of different spins from binary stars in elliptical and
                                        hyperbolic orbits. We perform the calculation using the field-theoretic method, in which the spectra of the angular
                                        momentum and radiated energy are derived in the frequency domain. This method enables us to compute the ratio
                                         ˙
                                        J(ω)/  Ṅ (ω) for a given frequency. For graviton, we find that this ratio deviates significantly from 2ℏ for the lower
                                        multipoles nω0 of the fundamental frequency and saturates to 2ℏ at large n.
                                           The loss of total angular momentum can be observed by measuring the change in eccentricity of the elliptical binary
                                        orbit with time [12]. The total angular momentum can be defined in terms of momentum and position operators for

                      V.    NON-GRAVITATIONAL EMISSION FROM BINARY STARS

   For calculating the energy radiation from binary stars in elliptical or hyperbolicporbits, we express the orbits in
Fourier space as a sum over the harmonics of the fundamental frequency ω0 = G(1 + α)M/a3 . Where in the
presence of a fifth force mediated by a vector exchange α = gV2 Q1 Q2 /(4πGm1 m2 ), when the orbital separation is
such that we have mV r ≪ 1. When the fifth force is mediated by a scalar exchange α = −gs2 N1 N2 /(4πGm1 m2 )
when ms r ≪ 1. the For compact binaries separated by distance a ≃ 1 A.U and masses mi ≃ 1.4 M⊙ the fundamental
frequency turns out to be ω0 ≃ 10−20 eV. This is in the mass range of ultralight dark matter [2–4]. In this section,
we compute the energy and angular momentum radiated by scalar dark matter like axions [9] and dark photons [9].
We then compute the angular momentum per quantum of radiation for the scalar and vector cases, as we did for
gravitational radiation, to examine whether the spin of radiated fields can be determined from observations.

                                                VII.   CONCLUSIONS

   Gravitational waves carry orbital and spin angular momentum of the graviton. Direct detection of GWs may probe
the angular momentum component of the GWs [30]. Another way to probe the angular momentum carried away
by GWs is through the observations of binary pulsars of the Hulse-Taylor [5] type. In this paper, we computed the
angular momentum loss of binary orbits due to graviton, scalar, and dark-photon radiation. We perform a field-
theoretic frequency-spectrum calculation of the energy and angular-momentum radiation for elliptical and hyperbolic
orbits.
   For elliptical orbits, the gravitational wave energy and angular momentum are calculated for each harmonic of the
fundamental frequency ω ′ = nω0 with n being a positive integer. While for hyperbolic orbits the frequency spectrum
ω ′ = νω0 with ν real and ν ∈ [0, ∞). We compute the energy and angular-momentum radiation rates of gravitons by
starting with linearized gravity and using Fermi’s golden rule. The rates of graviton emission from a classical source
are given by the amplitude squared |M|2 = |κT µν hµν |2 , summed over the final state phase space. For binaries in
an elliptical orbit, we reproduce the classical GR results [7, 12]. Keeping track of all factors of ℏ, we find that the
energy (Eq. (47)) and angular momentum (Eq. (61)) radiation rates are independent of ℏ. In our method, we can
separately compute the radiated orbital angular momentum (Eq. (55)) and spin angular momentum (Eq. (56)). We
find that the orbital angular momentum per graviton L̇/Ṅ ≃ (2/3)ℏ, while the spin angular momentum per graviton
is Ṡ/Ṅ ≃ (4/3)ℏ. Thus, the total angular momentum per graviton is J/     ˙ Ṅ ≃ 2ℏ. This ratio is also eccentricity
dependent, as we show in Fig. 3. The splitting of the graviton’s total angular momentum into spin and orbital parts
is not gauge-independent. Only the total radiated angular momentum can be inferred from the observations of binary
orbits. A recent paper by Page [1] uses the classical calculations of energy radiation [7] and the rate of angular
momentum loss [7] per orbit by elliptical binaries as gravitational waves, and showed that the ratio is J/   ˙ Ė = 2/ω0 .
Then, defining the graviton-number radiation rate per orbit as Ṅ ≡ Ė/(nℏω0 ), it was pointed out that the ratio

is to use observations of binary orbits to constrain the properties of these extra fifth-force particles [28, 32]. In this
paper, we extended this framework to calculate the angular momentum radiated via scalar and vector fields. This
allows us to explore whether observations can also be used to determine the spin of the additional radiated particles.
   We compute the energy loss due to the ultralight scalar radiation from the elliptical binary and obtain the closed-
form expression in Eq. (99). The angular momentum carried away by scalar radiation is given in Eq. (104). For
vector radiation, we derive the corresponding expressions for the energy and angular momentum losses in Eqs. (122)
and (127), respectively. We find that apart from the couplings and the extra degrees of freedom associated with the
vector fields, the expressions for the scalar and vector are identical in form. One can differentiate the scalar radiation
from vector radiation by measuring ė, ω̇0 from binary orbital observations and by using Eqs. (132) − (134), which
relate the energy and angular momentum losses to the orbital parameters and determine the sign of α. A positive
value of α from observations will provide evidence for a vector-mediated fifth force, whereas a negative value would
indicate a scalar-mediated fifth force.
