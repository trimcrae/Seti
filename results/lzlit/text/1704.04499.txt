                                                              The Milky Way dark matter halo is formed from the accretion of smaller subhalos. These sub-
                                                            units also harbor stars—typically old and metal-poor—that are deposited in the Galactic inner
                                                            regions by disruption events. In this Letter, we show that the dark matter and metal-poor stars
                                                            in the Solar neighborhood share similar kinematics due to their common origin. Using the high-
                                                            resolution Eris simulation, which traces the evolution of both the dark matter and baryons in a
arXiv:1704.04499v2 [astro-ph.GA] 12 Dec 2017




                                                            realistic Milky Way analog galaxy, we demonstrate that metal-poor stars are indeed effective tracers
                                                            for the local, virialized dark matter velocity distribution. The local dark matter velocities can
                                                            therefore be inferred from observations of the stellar halo made by the Sloan Digital Sky Survey
                                                            within 4 kpc of the Sun. This empirical distribution differs from the Standard Halo Model in
                                                            important ways and suggests that the bounds on the spin-independent scattering cross section may
                                                            be weakened for dark matter masses below ∼10 GeV. Data from Gaia will allow us to further refine
                                                            the expected distribution for the smooth dark matter component, and to test for the presence of
                                                            local substructure.

   This Supplementary Material reviews how the chemical abundances are determined in the Eris simulation [39, 42,
46, 47]. The Fe and O yields depend on both the initial mass function (IMF), which sets the number of Type Ia
and II supernovae (SNe), as well as the assumed chemical yields from each. The Eris yields are obtained following a
procedure similar to that of Ref. [60]. Each Ms = 6 × 103 M stellar particle in Eris represents its own population
of stars. The distribution of masses for the particle’s constituents are set by the Kroupa (1993) IMF [61]:
                                                         0.9 −1.3
                                                        2 m             0.08 ≤ m < 0.5 M
                           ΦKroupa (m) = Ms × NK × m−2.2                 0.5 ≤ m < 1 M                         (S1)
                                                         −2.7
                                                          m              m ≥ 1M ,

where
R      m is the stellar mass in units of M and NK = 0.303 is a normalization constant chosen such that
  m ΦKroupa dm = Ms M for 0.08 ≤ m ≤ 100 M .
  Stars with masses between 8 and 40 M explode as Type II SN, depositing iron and oxygen with mass yields

                                          MFe = 2.802 × 10−4 m1.864 M
                                           MO = 4.586 × 10−4 m2.721 M ,                                            (S2)

which follow the parametrization of Ref. [62]. Determining the number of Type Ia SN that are produced is more
involved—see Ref. [60] for a full description. Each Type Ia SN produces 0.63 M and 0.13 M of Fe and O,
respectively [63].
   Using the IMF and yields described above, we find that a single star particle in Eris produces 29.2 and 4.68 Type II
and Ia SNe, respectively. This results in 1.32 M of Fe and 28.5 M of O from Type II SN, as well as 2.95 M of Fe
and 0.608 M of O from Type Ia SN.
   The relative contribution of Fe and O depends on the assumptions made for the IMF and mass yields. Let us consider
how these values change taking the more modern Chabrier (2003) IMF [64] and the mass yields of Ref. [65, 66]. This
follows the prescription used for the Agora project [67]. The Chabrier mass function is

where mc = 0.08 M , σ = 0.69, and the normalization NC = 0.843 is chosen such that m ΦChabrier dm = 1 M for
0.1 ≤ m ≤ 100 M . In this case, 64.7 and 10.4 Type II and Ia SNe are produced per star particle, respectively. This

events. These assumptions yield 3.10 times more Fe and 2.81 times more O (per star particle) than the Eris values.
