                                                                                ABSTRACT
                                        New data relevant to the nature of Przybylski’s star, HD 101065, are presented. Photographic
                                      infrared observations of the hydrogen Paschen lines yield Teit = 7400 ± 300 K. Frequency analysis
                                      of photometric observations of over 100 cycles of the light variability of HD 101065 indicate a
                                      principal period of 12.141 ± 0.003 minutes. If this star is on the main sequence, a high pulsational
                                      overtone \k ^ 13) may be needed in order to explain the short period. Power spectrum analysis
                                      of the light variations indicates that HD 101065 is multiperiodic. It is suggested that an Ap star
                                      model can explain all of the known observations of HD 101065. It is argued that previous claims
                                      of Fe deficiency are not convincing.

                                                                           VI. INTERPRETATION OF THE DERIVED FREQUENCIES
                                                                            The interpretation of the frequencies derived in the
                                                                         last section is difficult. First, because the temperature
                                                                         implied by the Paschen lines in HD 101065 is 7400 K,
                                                                         which is on the cool border of the observed instability
                                                                         strip for 8 Scuti stars (Breger and Bregman 1975), we
                                                                         assume that the oscillations observed in HD 101065
                                                                         arise from the same physical mechanism as in the 8
                                                                         Scuti stars: the ionization of He n in the outer
                                                                         envelope of the star. That is to say, we assume that
                                                                         Przybylski’s star is a 8 Scuti star.
                                                                            The principal period of 12.141 minutes present in
                                                                         the variations of this star is virtually unprecedented for
                                                                         a 8 Scuti star. The shortest previously established
                                                                         periods for 8 Scuti stars are in the 30-40 minute range,
                                                                         although McGraw (private communication) reports a
                                                                         period of ~ 18 minutes for a southern 8 Scuti star and
                                                                         Kurtz (1979a) has reported variations on a time scale
                                                                         of 15-20 minutes in the variable 8 Delphini star
                                                                         HD 52788. The very short period observed in HD
                                                                         101065 argues that it pulsates in a relatively high
                                                                         overtone. The theoretical behavior of 8 Scuti stars
                                                                         pulsating in high overtones is not well studied (since
                                                                         such behavior has not been previously observed), but
                                                                         there is enough information in the literature to narrow
                                                                         down the possible pulsation modes.
