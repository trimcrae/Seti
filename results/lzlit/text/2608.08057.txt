                                    1. Introduction
   State-sum constructions of three-dimensional topological ﬁeld theories (TFTs) were
ﬁrst given by Turaev-Viro [TV92] and Barrett-Westbury [BW94]. State sums are natu-
rally understood in terms of open-closed TFTs which assign vector spaces to 2-manifolds
with boundary and linear maps to bordisms with corners, see for example the alterfold
theory [LMW+ 23a, LMW+ 23b, LMW+ 24a], as well as [SS25]. The constructions men-
tioned so far all require semisimple algebraic input.
   Recently, there has been a vast development in the construction of TFTs based on
non-semisimple algebraic inputs, with the ﬁrst major step forward being the invari-
ants in [CGP12] and the corresponding TFT in [BCGP14]. Further examples of non-
semisimple 3d TFTs include the one constructed from modular tensor categories based
on the surgery presentation of 3-manifolds [DGG+ 19], as well as the one deﬁned in
[CGPV23] from chromatic categories (generalization of spherical fusion categories) via
admissible skein modules and the generators-and-relations presentation of bordism cat-
egories in [Juh14].

   In this paper, we construct a non-semisimple open-closed TFT, whose algebraic input
is a triple (C, t, π1 ) where C is a spherical ﬁnite tensor category, t is a non-zero modiﬁed
trace on ProjC and π1 : P1 → 1 is a surjection which just serves as normalisation. Our
construction crucially uses chromatic maps and their properties from [CGPV23], as well
as the corresponding invariant F of admissible bichrome graphs in multi-handlebodies
deﬁned in [CGPT18]. The basic idea is as follows: We ﬁrst extend the multi-handlebody
invariant F to an invariant τC of general 3-manifolds with boundary, together with a C-
coloured bulk-admissible graph embedded in the boundary. Bulk-admissibility means
that each connected component of the 3-manifold has a projectively labelled edge some-
where in its boundary (Deﬁnition 4.4). In particular, a connected 3-manifold is not
allowed to have empty boundary. We then use the universal construction [BHMV95] to
deﬁne a TFT.
   We brieﬂy outline the deﬁnition of the invariant τC (M, Γ), where M is a 3-manifold
with boundary and Γ is a bulk-admissible C-graph in ∂M . We choose a PLCW decom-
position of M and push Γ to the boundary of a tubular neighbourhood of the 1-skeleton,
resulting in a multi-handlebody H with an admissible C-graph on its boundary. Next,
we construct a graph using the 2- and 3-cells (Deﬁnition 4.1), and choose a spanning
tree of the graph. Then, we add red loops to H to record how 2-cells are attached to
the 1-skeleton of M , where we only add red loops for 2-cells that do not correspond to
the edges in the spanning tree. In this way, H is decorated with an admissible bichrome
(red and blue) graph Γ′ on its boundary surface, and we deﬁne τC (M, Γ) to be the
multi-handlebody invariant F(H, Γ′ ).
   The ﬁrst main result of this paper is that τC (M, Γ) does not depend on the choices of
the PLCW decomposition, the spanning tree, and how Γ is pushed to the boundary of
the handlebody H. Namely, we have


Theorem 1.1 (Theorem 4.8). τC (M, Γ) is well-deﬁned and only depends on the diﬀeo-
morphism class of (M, Γ).

   The key novelty of our construction is the use of spanning trees when adding red loops.
Based on existing state-sum constructions of 3-manifold invariants and TFTs, it might be
tempting to add red loops for every 2-cell: Indeed, in the semisimple case, this is typical
(see for example [LMW+ 23a] and [SS25]); and in the non-semisimple case, this is closely
related what the authors of [CGPT18] did to deﬁne an invariant of closed 3-manifolds.
However, in the presence of boundaries, such a construction would often create red
loops bounding disks after handle-slides/sliding moves, and such red loops evaluate to
zero in many non-semisimple cases (Remark 2.7), but also in some semisimple examples
(Remark 2.8). In our construction we avoid such obviously unwanted zeros by using
spanning trees, resulting in not only fewer red loops, but also less room for the red loops
to slide (cf. the proof of Lemma 4.13).
   One can deﬁne an invariant of closed connected 3-manifolds M by evaluating τC on
M minus a 3-ball with a suitable admissible graph I on the boundary S2 (see Section 4.3
for details). In Proposition 4.11 we show that τC (M \ B3 , I) agrees with the invariant
KC (M ) of closed connected 3-manifolds in [CGPT18].
  After deﬁning the invariant τC , we use the universal construction to construct a non-
compact open-closed TFT on the bordism category of surfaces whose boundaries contain
C-marked points (called ∂-marked surfaces), and 3-dimensional bordisms between them:

Theorem 1.2 (Theorem 5.10). Via the universal construction, the invariant τC extends
to an open-closed topological ﬁeld theory V : Bordnc
                                                  oc (C) → Vectk .

  The key to the proof of Theorem 5.10 is Proposition 5.8, which says that the state
space of any ∂-marked surface (Σ, L) is spanned by what we call “collar elements”. Such
an element has underlying 3-manifold Σ = Σ × [0, 1] viewed as a bordism from ∅ to
(Σ, L). Using collar elements, we show that there exists a surjection
                             E(Σ, L) : Sadm (Σ, L) → V(Σ, L)
from the admissible skein module of (Σ, L) to the corresponding state space of V (Propo-
sition 5.9). In particular, V(Σ, L) is ﬁnite-dimensional. In addition to the ﬁnite-
dimensionality of the state spaces, Proposition 5.8 also helps us to establish monoidality
of V.
   Both the TFT V in this paper and the TFT S of admissible skein modules in [CGPV23]
depend heavily on the properties of the multi-handlebody invariant F, and the source
bordism category of S can be identiﬁed with the full subcategory of Bordnc oc (C) consisting
of closed surfaces with no marked points. However, the two TFTs are deﬁned by diﬀerent
methods: V is deﬁned via the universal construction, while S is deﬁned by Juhász’s
presentation of bordism categories [Juh14]. We expect that V agrees with S for closed
surfaces and admissible bordisms with empty free boundary (Remark 5.12).
   When the input category C is semisimple, we argue in Remark 4.12 that τC generalises
the Turaev-Viro invariant by comparing both of these invariants with the alterfold in-
variant ZC in [LMW+ 23a]. Namely, τC is a non-zero scalar multiple of ZC and, since both
the open-closed TFT V and the alterfold TFT are built from the universal construction,
the state space of any 2-alterfold under the alterfold TFT is isomorphic to the state
space of its B-coloured region (which is a ∂-marked surface) under V, see Remark 5.13.

  Finally, it would be interesting to explore the relation between the TFT V in this
paper and the TFT constructed in [DGG+ 19]. We expect that for any spherical ﬁnite
tensor category C, our TFT V coincide with the TFT in [DGG+ 19] associated to the
Drinfeld center Z(C) of C. This is inspired by the general “RT=TV” philosophy in the
semisimple case [TV17, LMW+ 23b], which is supported in particular by the alterfold
theory. We plan to return to this question in the future.

   The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2, we review the deﬁnition and basic
properties of the algebraic data that is used to deﬁne the 3-manifold invariant τC and
the topological V. In Section 3, we review admissible skein modules and the handlebody
invariant F. In Section 4, we give the deﬁnition of the invariant τC and prove its topo-
logical invariance. Finally, in Section 5, we apply the universal construction to construct
V, and verify that it is indeed a topological ﬁeld theory.

3.1. Admissible skein modules. In this section, we recall the deﬁnition of admissi-
ble skein modules, introduced in [CGPV23], and in [RW] under the name non-unital
skein modules. For a general discussion of admissible skein modules, see also [RST24].
Roughly speaking, the skein module of a surface is the vector space freely spanned by
C-coloured graphs modulo relations induced by C which hold inside embedded disks.
If we require each graph to have at least one projectively coloured edge, we speak of
admissible graphs and admissible skein modules.
  Let us go through the deﬁnition in more detail. We ﬁrst need the notion of a ∂-marked
surface.
Deﬁnition 3.1. A ∂-marked surface (Σ, L) (over C) is a compact2 oriented surface Σ
with boundary ∂Σ (possibly empty), and a ﬁnite set L of marked points. A marked
point is a triple (p, ν, X) where p ∈ ∂Σ, ν ∈ {±}, and X ∈ C. The points p in L are
required to be mutually distinct, i.e., the map L → ∂Σ is injective.
   Let (Σ, L) be a ∂-marked surface over C. A blue graph (over C), or a (blue) C-graph,
in (Σ, L), is a union of a ﬁnite collection of oriented arcs (embedded [0, 1]), circles
(embedded S1 ) and vertices (embedded points). We refer to the arcs and circles as edges
of the blue graph. The vertices, circles and the interior of the arcs (images of (0, 1))
are mutually disjoint, and are contained in the interior of Σ. The endpoints of the arcs
either lie on vertices of Γ or on marked points on ∂Σ. In the latter case, the arc meets
∂Σ transversally. Each arc and each circle is labelled by an object of C. The endpoints
of the arcs meeting a vertex v are cyclically ordered by the orientation of Σ, and are
partitioned into two consecutive sets of ingoing and outgoing endpoints, both equipped
with a total order compatible with the cyclic order. The vertex is labelled by a morphism
in C, compatible with the in– and outgoing endpoints of edges, their orientation, and
their labels. It will be convenient to represent the vertices as coupons, as in the usual
string diagram notation:

   A generalised n-cell A(n) is the image of a map χ : Dn → RN such that χ(Int(Dn )) ∩

                5. Open-closed TFT via the universal construction
5.1. Bulk-admissible bordisms. Before deﬁning the relevant bordism categories, we
brieﬂy set up the notations and recall some basic concepts on bordisms. For the rest
of the paper, we assume that all manifolds are compact and oriented, and that for all
n ∈ N, any embedding of an (n − 1)-manifold into an n-manifold is equipped with a
collar, which we usually suppress for simplicity.

Deﬁnition 5.1. Suppose Σ, Σ′ are compact oriented surface with boundaries O = ∂Σ
and O ′ = ∂Σ′ . A bordism from Σ to Σ′ is a compact oriented 3-manifold M with corners.
The boundary ∂M is equipped with a decomposition into two parts, the free boundary
∂ frM and the gluing boundary ∂ glM such that
     • ∂M = ∂ frM ∪ ∂ glM , and
     • ∂ glM ∩ ∂ frM is precisely the set of corner points of M .
Furthermore, M is equipped with an orientation preserving diﬀeomorphism −Σ ⊔ Σ′ →
∂ glM (the gluing boundary parametrisation) such that O⊔O ′ is mapped onto ∂ glM ∩∂ frM .
We call Σ and Σ′ the source and target of M , and we write M : Σ → Σ′ .

  Note that the above decomposition of ∂M endows M with the structure of a 3-
dimensional h2i-manifold (see for example [LP05, HR24]).
                          NON-SEMISIMPLE OPEN-CLOSED 3D TFT                                 39

   Let (C, t, π1 ) be as in (2.4), and let (Σ, L) and (Σ′ , L′ ) be ∂-marked surfaces (Deﬁni-
tion 3.1) over C. An orientation-preserving diﬀeomorphism from (Σ, L) to (Σ′ , L′ ) is an
orientation-preserving diﬀeomorphism f : Σ → Σ′ such that f (∂Σ) = ∂Σ′ , f (L) = L′ ,
and for any marked point x ∈ L, its label equals to the label of f (x) ∈ L′ . In the
following, all diﬀeomorphisms are assumed to be orientation-preserving, and by abuse
of notation, we write f : (Σ, L) → (Σ′ , L′ ).

Deﬁnition 5.2. Let (C, t, π1 ) be as in (2.4), and let (Σ, L), (Σ′ , L′ ) be ∂-marked surfaces
over C.
  (1) A bordism of ∂-marked surfaces from (Σ, L) to (Σ′ , L′ ) is a pair (M, Γ), where
      M : Σ → Σ′ is a bordism of compact oriented surfaces, and Γ is a C-graph in the
      ∂-marked surface (∂ frM , −L ⊔ L′ ). We write (M, Γ) : (Σ, L) → (Σ′ , L′ ) and call
      (Σ, L) and (Σ′ , L′ ) the source and target of (M, Γ) respectively.
  (2) A bordism (M, Γ) : (Σ, L) → (Σ′ , L′ ) of ∂-marked surfaces is called bulk-admissible
      if it satisﬁes the following condition: For each connected component J of M that
      is disjoint from the input surface Σ, there is at least one blue edge of Γ that is
      contained in ∂J and is labelled by an object in ProjC . In this case, we will also
      call Γ a bulk-admissible C-graph in the bordism (M, Γ).

Remark 5.3. The bulk-admissibility condition in Deﬁnition 5.2 can be viewed as a
generalization of that in Deﬁnition 4.4. Let (M, Γ) : ∅ → ∅ be a bordism from the
empty set to itself. Such a bordism is also called a closed bordism. In particular, we
have ∂ glM = ∅, ∂ frM = ∂M , and Γ ⊂ ∂M is a graph with no free ends. In this case,
(M, Γ) is a bulk-admissible bordism in the sense of Deﬁnition 5.2 if and only if Γ is a
bulk-admissible C-graph in M in the sense of Deﬁnition 4.4.
  Note also that if we glue two bulk-admissible bordisms of ∂-marked surfaces along
their common gluing boundaries, then the resulting bordism is again bulk-admissible.

   Let (M, Γ) and (M ′ , Γ′ ) : (Σ, L) → (Σ′ , L′ ) be two bulk-admissible bordisms of ∂-
marked surfaces. An orientation preserving diﬀeomorphism ϕ : M → M ′ is called an
isomorphism from (M, Γ) to (M ′ , Γ′ ) if ϕ preserves the embeddings of −Σ ∪ Σ′ into M
and M ′ respectively, and ϕ(Γ) = Γ′ . We call the isomorphism class of (M, Γ) : (Σ, L) →
(Σ′ , L′ ) the bordism class of (M, Γ), and we denote it by [M, Γ] : (Σ, L) → (Σ′ , L′ ) or
simply [M, Γ].
   Note that ∂-marked surfaces, with bulk-admissible bordism classes between them as
morphisms, form a category: It is routine to show that the composition of bordism classes
by gluing representatives of bordism classes along their common gluing boundaries is
well-deﬁned and associative. The identity morphism of (Σ, L) is simply (Σ × [0, 1], L ×
[0, 1]), where the lines L × [0, 1] are coloured in blue and labelled by the same object as
their corresponding end points. Moreover, this category is symmetric monoidal under
disjoint union.

Deﬁnition 5.4. Let (C, t, π1 ) be as in (2.4). The category of 3-dimensional non-compact
open-closed bordisms over C, denoted by Bordnc  oc (C), is the symmetric monoidal category
whose objects are ∂-marked surfaces over C, and whose morphisms are bulk-admissible
bordism classes between ∂-marked surfaces over C. A symmetric monoidal functor from
40                              INGO RUNKEL AND YILONG WANG

Bordncoc (C) to a symmetric monoidal category is called a non-compact open-closed topo-
