Received YYY; in original form ZZZ 2015 document firstpage -- lastpage abstract We study the origin of the interstellar object 1I/2017 U1 by juxtaposing estimates based on the observations with simulations. We speculate that objects like are formed in the debris disc as left over from the star and planet formation process, and subsequently liberated. The liberation process is mediated either by interaction with other stars in the parental star-cluster, by resonant interactions within the planetesimal disc or by the relatively sudden mass loss when the host star becomes a compact object. Integrating backward in time in the Galactic potential together with stars from the Gaia-TGAS catalogue we find that about 1.3\,Myr ago passed the nearby star HIP 17288 within a mean distance of 1.3 \,pc. By comparing nearby observed L-dwarfs with simulations of the Galaxy we conclude that the kinematics of is consistent with relatively young objects of 1.1 -- 1.7 \,Gyr. Most notable orbital parameters are the distance at pericentre, q = 0.25534 0.00007 \,au, the eccentricity, e = 1.1995 0.0002 \, and the relative velocity at infinity v 26.32 0.01 \,km/s with respect to the Sun . This makes the object unambiguously unbound on an en-passant orbit through the Solar system , not unlike the prediction by . The absence of a tail indicates that the object is probably rock-like , which, together with its unusual elongated shape [ 35 by 230\,m reported by][and explained by and ] 2017Natur.552..378M and rapid spin [with an 8.10 0.02 hour period, see][] 2017ApJ...850L..36J,2018ApJ...852L...2B , has been hosted by a star for an extensive period of time . In this period it may have lost most of its volatiles and ice by sputtering . By the lack of other terminology for this class of objects and for convenience, we refer in this work to , which is Latin for ``lonely stone''. Spectra taken by several instruments indicate that its colour ( g-r = 0.2 0.4 and r-i = 0.3 0.3 , g-r =0.41 0.24 and r-i=0.23 0.25 , and g'-r' = 0.60 0.23 ) matches that of objects in the Kuiper belt but that it is somewhat red compared to the main belt asteroids and trojans [see also][] 2017ApJ...851L..38B,2018AJ....155...56J . However, it cannot be excluded that is the left over nucleus of a comet from another star . Based on its velocity, is unlikely to have originated from a nearby star , although argue that it could have been ejected with a low velocity (of 1 -- 2 \,km/s) from the nearby young stellar associations Carina or Columba or from the Pleiades moving group . Tracing back the trajectory of \, through the local neighbourhood is hindered by the magnification of small uncertainties in its orbit and in the positions and velocities of nearby stars . Even with the high accuracy achieved by Gaia and TGAS, backtracing its orbit can only be done reliably for some 10\,Myr or over a distance of about 30\,pc . Since we doubt that formed as an isolated object, it seems most plausible that it formed around another star, from which it was liberated. This could be the result of copious mass loss resulting from the end phase of the nuclear burning of a star , by a close encounter in a young star cluster , or by internal scattering of planetary systems or in a binary star . It is even possible that one of the Sun's own Oort cloud objects was scattered by a yet unknown planet . Here we discuss the possible origin of (see \, ). We analyse its orbital properties and compare those with our expectations for a random population of free-floating debris in the solar neighbourhood (see \, ). Our estimates of the occurrence of objects similar to \, is juxtaposed with several alternative scenarios. In the near future when the Large Synoptic Telescope comes online many more are expected to be found at a rate of 2 to 12 per year. Dynamical ejection from the Sun's Oort cloud Considering the orbital parameters of , it seems unlikely to have been launched from the Kuiper belt or Oort cloud . Asteroids and comets are frequently launched from the Kuiper belt and the Oort cloud but they rarely penetrate the inner Solar system . Objects in the Oort cloud, however, could possibly launch into the inner Solar system after a strong interaction with a passing star or a planet . The highest velocity, in this case, is obtained when the object would have been the member of a close binary with another, more massive, rocky object; much in the same way in which hypervelocity stars are ejected from the supermassive black hole in the Galactic centre . Binaries are known to be common in the outer regions in the Solar system, and possibly the majority of planetesimals in the Kuiper belt formed as pairs . The mean orbital separation of 13 trans-Neptunian objects is 13900 14000 \,km with a minimum of 1830 \,km for the pair Ceto/Phorcys (object \#65489), but there is no particular reason why Oort cloud or Kuiper-belt binaries could not have tighter orbits , such as is suspected of 2001 QG298 . An interaction of an object much like the binary (136108) 2003 EL61 with a planet with ten times the mass of Jupiter could result in a runaway velocity of the least massive member of 1 \,km/s, but with the parameters for Ceto/Phorcys the mean kick velocity of 0.13 \,km/s. This would be sufficient to change a circular orbit at a distance of 1000 -- 10^4 \,au around the Sun into an unbound orbit with the eccentricity observed for . We perform Monte Carlo experiments in which we distribute Oort cloud objects around the Sun using the parameters adopted in . We introduce a velocity kick v_ kick , taken randomly from a Gaussian distribution with dispersion of 0.13\,km/s in each of the Cartesian directions, which results in a wide distribution in semi-major axis and eccentricity. More than 85\% of the objects remain bound and the remaining 15\% unbound objects tend to have high eccentricities with a median of 1.2 , but only a very small fraction of 10^ -3 objects pass the Sun within 100\,au on their unbound orbit [see also][] 2017Ap&SS.362..198D . We subsequently performed several direct N -body simulations to verify this result. For these we adopted the connected-component symplectic integrator Huayno within the AMUSE framework. The mild kicks expected due to an interaction with an Oort cloud binary and a massive planet, either passing through or bound to the Oort cloud, are insufficient to explain the observed velocity of . We, therefore, conclude that is unlikely to have originated from either the Kuiper belt or the Oort cloud [see also][] 2018MNRAS.476L...1D . An origin from the solar neighbourhood Results by the provide the most complete and accurate census of the distribution of stars in the solar neighbourhood (for which we adopt a volume centred around the Sun with a radius of 50\,pc). The radial velocity components are not (yet) part of this database, but they can be completed using other catalogues. By matching the TGAS catalogue with the radial velocities from , Pulkovo , and Geneva-Copenhagen , we constructed a catalogue of 270,664 stars with position and velocity information , we selected those stars with relative proper motions and parallax errors <1 \,\%, with radial velocity <100 \,km/s, with errors <10 \,km/s [see also][] 2018ApJ...852L..27F . Discussion and conclusions Based on the existence of , we derive a local density of 3.5 10^ 13 -- 2.1 10^ 15 \,pc ^ -3 (0.0040--0.24\,au ^ -3 ). This is a high density, but in line with other estimates . It is consistent with the amount of debris ejected during the star and planet formation process, but inconsistent with expulsion of exo Oort-cloud or asteroidal objects (see \, ). If each star contributes to the formation of then the entire Galaxy may be swarming with such objects, with O ( 10^ 23 ) in the Milky Way. Once liberated from their parent stars, it is quite possible that such an object grazes any other star, much in the same way passed close to the Sun. The velocity of is low compared to the stars in the Gaia-TGAS catalogue, and low compared to the mean velocity distribution of L-dwarfs. Young L-dwarfs have a considerably lower velocity than their older siblings, and the velocity of is consistent with the youngest population of brown dwarfs (1.1--1.7\,Gyr). Based on its velocity, we argue that has a similar age [see also][] 2018ApJ...852L..27F . Earlier estimates of the interstellar asteroid density were carried out to explain the daily X-ray flares on the super-massive black-hole in the Galactic centre Sgr A . They argue that the daily X-ray flares in the direction of Sgr A can be explained with a local density of 10^ 14 asteroids pc ^ -3 , which is consistent with the density of derived here. We conclude that is part of the left-over debris of the star and planet formation process in the Galaxy. We expect that the Galaxy is rich in such objects, with a density of 10^ 14 or 10^ 15 objects per cubic parsec. We estimate the probability that a passes the Sun within 1\,au, taking the gravitational focusing corrected cross-section into account, at an event rate of about 2--12 per year.
