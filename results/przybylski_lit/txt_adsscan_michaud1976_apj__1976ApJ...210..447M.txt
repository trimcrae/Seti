                                                      ABSTRACT
              The abundance anomalies generated by diffusion in the envelope of main-sequence stars are
           studied. It is shown that in low-mass stars (M ^1.2 M0) diffusion leads to underabundances
           while in more massive stars (M ^ 1.3 AfG) diffusion leads to overabundances of at least some
           elements. In general the overabundance and underabundance factors generated (up to 107) are
           larger than the observed anomalies in stars of the main sequence (rarely up to 106). It is established
           that diffusion can lead to the largest anomalies observed. For particular elements (Sr, Eu, ...), it
           is shown where more accurate calculations are needed. Approximate formulae are developed for
           radiative accelerations. They allow the reader to carry out calculations for cases of special interest
           to him and also to evaluate the uncertainty of the calculations.
           Subject headings: stars: abundances — stars: interiors — stars: metallic-line — stars: peculiar A
