                                                                                    Abstract
                                              Ideal attached points are a core concept in general relativity for pseudo-
                                              Riemannian manifolds, and whether the spacetime can be extended with certain
                                              properties is a central consideration in their choice. This paper establishes a
                                              sufficient condition for the separability between singularities and points at infin-
                                              ity for any maximally extended pseudo-Riemannian manifold. We focus on the
                                              incomplete geodesics of (M, g), and produce an envelopment (M, g, M̂) of the
                                              spacetime such that an incomplete geodesic γ : [0, 1) → M has an endpoint q in
                                              M̂. If there is no pair of geodesics approaching q which is intertwined, then q is
                                              a singularity. Additionally, q will not be approached by any geodesic with infinite
                                              affine parameter, and therefore cannot cover a point at infinity, thereby rendering
                                              it a pure singularity in the abstract boundary framework. We apply the Endpoint
                                              Theorem to the maximal g-boundary introduced by Graf and Beld-Serrano in [1],
                                              and also provide a result on the separability between directional singularities and
                                              pure singularities. This analysis is then applied to the Schwarzschild spacetime.

                                              Keywords: abstract boundary construction, pseudo-Riemannian geometry,
                                              envelopment, maximal extension, singularity, region at infinity, separability




                                         1 Introduction
                                         In general relativity one often seeks to extend spacetimes under consideration. The
                                         extension of a spacetime is intimately connected to the question of how to attach a
                                         boundary to a spacetime. The topological structure of singularities related to geodesic
                                         incompleteness has been widely explored via Geroch’s g-boundary [2], Schmidt’s
                                         b-boundary [3], Geroch, Kronheimer and Penrose’s c-boundary [4, 5], and Scott and


                                                                                         1
Szekeres’s abstract boundary (a-boundary) [6]. The a-boundary is a flexible scheme
to classify boundary points under different embeddings of spacetime.

    For many interesting results related to globally hyperbolic spacetimes and the
vacuum Einstein field equation, asymptotically flat initial data is assumed as a
physical condition. It is an interesting question as to whether the regions at infinity
and the singularities should be disjoint in this framework. It remains an important
open question, in general, as to how to separate singularities and points at infinity in
embeddings? This question has a well-framed description in the abstract boundary
context, namely the issue of the separability between pure singularities and pure
points at infinity (i.e. whether there exist open neighbourhoods of the boundary
points of M in embeddings ψ1 : M → M̂ and ψ2 : M → M′ such that these open
neighbourhoods of the pure singularity and the pure point at infinity respectively,
restricted to M, are disjoint).

    Using the abstract boundary construction, in this paper we will only consider
embeddings ψ : M → M̂ of n-dimensional, pseudo-Riemannian manifolds (M, g )
which are maximally extended. In relation to the singularity theorems, this involves
no loss of generality, as they also assume that the spacetime is maximally extended [7].

    For the abstract boundary construction, if there exists an embedding ψ : M → M̂
and p ∈ ∂ψ (M) such that p is only approached by curves with an unbounded affine
parameter, we say that p is a point at infinity. For a singularity, if there exists an
embedding ψ : M → M̂ and p ∈ ∂ψ (M) such that p is approached by some curves
with bounded affine parameter, then p is an essential singularity [6]. A pure singularity
is an essential singularity that cannot be re-embedded into a non-singular boundary
point in any other embedding. The relevant details of the abstract boundary will be
introduced formally in Section 2. The following theorem provides the motivation for
us to directly investigate maximally extended pseudo-Riemannian manifolds.

Theorem 1.1. For a maximally extended pseudo-Riemannian manifold (M, g ),
boundary points of envelopments of (M, g ) have the following properties:
1. A point at infinity must be a pure point at infinity
2. A singularity can only be either a directional singularity or a pure singularity
3. A directional singularity must cover a pure point at infinity
4. A pure point at infinity/pure singularity can only cover pure points at infinity/pure
   singularities, respectively, and non-regular unapproachable boundary points.


Proof The core assumption is that (M, g) is maximally extended so that there does not exist
any extension (M′ , g ′ ) of (M, g), and thus there are no regular boundary points.      □

   For any maximally extended pseudo-Riemannian manifold, Theorem 1.1 simplifies
the separability problem between directional singularities and pure singularities to
the separability problem between pure points at infinity and pure singularities. The
question then reduces to the asymptotic behaviour of some specific curves with finite


                                            2
length and some with infinite length. The Endpoint Theorem [8] is essential for the
solution of this question.

Theorem 1.2. (The Endpoint Theorem) Let M be an n-dimensional, smooth, con-
nected, Hausdorff, paracompact manifold. If (xi )i∈N is a sequence of points in M
without an accumulation point, then there always exists an n-dimensional, smooth,
connected, Hausdorff, paracompact manifold N and an open embedding ψ : M → N ,
such that ∂ψ (M) is diffeomorphic to the n − 1 dimensional unit ball and the sequence
(ψ (xi ))i∈N converges to some y ∈ ∂ψ (M).

   The exhaustion method used in the proof of the Endpoint Theorem [8] works for
any topological manifold (that is, any manifold with C l charts for all l ≥ 0). The
normal neighbourhood construction employed requires the existence of geodesics, so
the Endpoint Theorem is applicable to any C k manifold for all k ≥ 2.

   For any sequence of points without an accumulation point in an n-dimensional
manifold M, the Endpoint Theorem constructs an embedding of M into a larger n-
dimensional manifold N such that the sequence has an accumulation point, indeed an
endpoint, in the boundary set of this embedding. It does not only take into account
how the geometry is extended through the boundary, but also how the boundary is
globally attached to the manifold.

1.1 Main results and application to the maximal g-boundary
For the normal neighbourhood construction in the proof of Theorem 1.2, one has
the flexibility to adjust the neighbourhood via the size function f : [0, 1) → R+ (a
smooth function). A sufficiently small normal neighbourhood U of a geodesic can be
chosen such that the geodesic has an endpoint in the new embedding which is not
the endpoint of any other geodesic. This technique allows us to restrict geodesics
approaching the targeted boundary point in N . With the abstract boundary classi-
fication for any maximally extended pseudo-Riemannian manifold (M, g ), it implies
that the endpoint in N of the central geodesic with bounded affine parameter is very
likely to be a pure singularity.

    Limiting behaviour, however, also plays an essential role in this classification. There
exist some types of curves which cannot be isolated by adjusting the size function,
leading to these geodesics sharing the common limit point with the central geodesic
in a specific embedding. We introduce a concept called intertwining, first discussed by
Chruściel in [9]. Using an envelopment from Theorem 1.2, we found that the endpoint
of the geodesic is a pure singularity if it is not intertwined with any other geodesic
in M. The following proposition (Proposition 4.10 in Section 4) shows that the inter-
twined geodesic condition provides a clear delineation between a pure singularity and
a pure point at infinity.

Proposition 1.3. Let (M, g, M̂, ψ, C ) be an envelopment of a maximally extended
pseudo-Riemannian manifold (M, g ), where C is the class of geodesics with affine

parameter, with p ∈ ∂ (ψ (M)) a pure singularity, and γ : [0, b) → M (where b ∈
R+ ∪{+∞}) is a non-self-intersecting geodesic in C without limit points in M. Suppose
ψ (γ ) → p. By Proposition 3.2, there exists another embedding ψ ′ such that ψ ′ (γ ) →
q ∈ ∂ (ψ ′ (M)), p ▷ q and no other geodesic in C ends at q . Suppose that no pair of
geodesics in C , each of which approaches q , is intertwined in (M, g, M̂′ , ψ ′ , C ). Then
b ∈ R+ (the geodesic γ has bounded affine parameter).

   The important differences between Proposition 4.10 and the relevant results in [1]
and [9] are:
1. The technique works for a metric which is at least C 2,1
2. The target of the technique is geodesics in a pseudo-Riemannian manifold
3. An intertwined geodesic is neither a completely global nor local concept. This paper
   assumes a specific geodesic not intertwined with any other geodesics in M instead
   of no intertwined geodesics in M.
Based on Proposition 1.3, we discuss the properties of a pure singularity and its dif-
ference from a directional singularity with respect to curve asymptotic behaviour. In
Section 4.3 we explore the maximal g-boundary introduced by Graf and Beld-Serrano
in [1] using the abstract boundary framework. Inspired by the intertwining concept,
in Section 5 we test whether the Penrose diagram for the Schwarzschild spacetime
is an abstract boundary optimal embedding. This example exemplifies an abstract
boundary physical application which connects the purity of the singularity with the
curvature singularity, and the inextendibility of the Schwarzschild spacetime (i.e.,
the curvature singularity in the interior of the Schwarzschild spacetime is a pure
singularity, and is C 0 inextendible [10].)

    The paper is organised as follows. Section 2 provides a brief review of the
background for the abstract boundary construction. Section 3 presents the core
application of the Endpoint Theorem by controlling the size of the normal neigh-
bourhood: we show in Proposition 3.2 that there exists an embedding ψ constructed
along a geodesic λ without accumulation points in M such that no other geodesic
γ in M ends at the endpoint of λ in ψ . Section 4 delves into the definition of inter-
twined geodesics under the abstract boundary framework and the structure of pure
singularities. We investigate the separability between pure singularities and pure
points at infinity if the geodesic without accumulation points in M is not intertwined
with any other geodesics in the newly constructed embedding. In Section 4.2, we
briefly explore an application of Proposition 4.10 to the existence of a minimal pure
singularity, and we further show the application of Proposition 4.10 to the maximal
g-boundary in Section 4.3. In Section 5 the non-intertwined geodesic behaviour of the
Schwarzschild spacetime is derived and we show that the Penrose maximal extension
of the Schwarzschild spacetime is an abstract boundary optimal embedding [11, 12].
We also examine Wheeler’s definition of a black hole and event horizon [13] in relation
to this optimal embedding.

2 Reviewing the Abstract Boundary
The core construction of the abstract boundary can be carried out for any pseudo-
Riemannian manifold. Unless otherwise specified, the manifolds considered will always
be smooth, connected, Hausdorff, paracompact and without boundaries. In all cases,
we denote a for a given index number, and γ a means that we consider a specific a
component for γ . Note that λ(t) → q follows the abstract boundary notation i.e. q
is the endpoint of the curve λ(t) : [0, 1) → M. We denote {p} ▷ {q} by p ▷ q for
convenience. We also adopt the Einstein summation convention in which repeated
indices are implicitly summed.

2.1 The core definitions
The introduction of the abstract boundary is originally from [6].

Definition 2.1. A point p ∈ M is a limit point of a parametrised curve γ : [a, b) → M
if there is an increasing infinite sequence of real numbers ti → b such that γ (ti ) →
p. Moreover, a point p ∈ M is an endpoint of a parametrised curve γ if for every
increasing infinite sequence of real numbers ti → b, γ (ti ) → p.

Definition 2.2. An envelopment is a triple (M, M̂, ϕ) where M and M̂ are differ-
entiable manifolds of the same dimension n and ϕ is an open embedding ϕ : M →
M̂.

Definition 2.3. A boundary point p of an envelopment (M, M̂, ϕ) is a point p ∈
M̂\ϕ(M) such that every open neighbourhood U of p in M̂ has non-empty intersection
with ϕ(M).

Definition 2.4. (Covering Boundary Sets)
Let (M, M̂, ϕ, B ) and (M, M′ , ϕ′ , B ′ ) be two envelopments for M with boundary sets
B and B ′ respectively. Then the boundary set B is said to cover the boundary set B ′
if for every open neighbourhood U of B in M̂, there exists an open neighbourhood U ′
of B ′ in M′ such that

                              ϕ ◦ ϕ′−1 (U ′ ∩ ϕ′ (M)) ⊆ U.                          (1)

Definition 2.5. (Equivalent Boundary Sets)
Let (M, M̂, ϕ, B ) and (M, M′ , ϕ′ , B ′ ) be two enveloped manifolds with boundary sets
B and B ′ respectively. Then B is said to be equivalent to B ′ (B ∼ B ′ ) if and only if
B covers B ′ and B ′ covers B .

2.2 Classifying abstract boundary points
Definition 2.6. (The a-boundary B (M) )
If p is a boundary point of an envelopment (M, M̂, ϕ), then the equivalence class [p]
of the boundary set {p} under the equivalence relation ∼ of Definition 2.5 is called an
abstract boundary point of M. The set B (M) of all such abstract boundary points


                                           5
for all envelopments of the given manifold M is called the abstract boundary or the
a-boundary.

     A class C of curves in M satisfies the bounded parameter property if (i) through
every point of M passes at least one curve in C , (ii) every subcurve is a curve in C ,
(iii) if two curves in C are related by a change of parameter, then their parameters
are either both bounded or are both unbounded. Given an envelopment (M, M̂, ϕ), a
boundary point p ∈ ∂ϕ(M) is called a C -boundary point (or approachable) if it is a
limit point of some curve in C .

Definition 2.7. (C l Singular Boundary Point)
A boundary point p of an envelopment (M, g, C, M̂, ϕ) is said to be C l singular if
1. p is not a C l regular boundary point,
2. p is a C -boundary point, and
3. there exists a curve in the family C which approaches p with bounded parameter.

   We note that a boundary point p is called a C l point at infinity if it satisfies
conditions 1 and 2 above with an additional condition that no curve in C approaches
p with bounded parameter.

    A boundary point p of an envelopment (M, g, C, M̂, ϕ) is called a C l essential
singularity if it is a C l singular boundary point which is not covered by a C l non-
singular boundary set B (i.e. a boundary set consisting only of points which are all
either C l regular, C l points at infinity or unapproachable boundary points).

    We now proceed to the definitions of a directional singularity and a pure singu-
larity which are the core concepts required for the results of this paper.


Definition 2.8. An essential singularity p is called a mixed or directional singularity
if p covers a boundary point q which is either regular or a point at infinity. Otherwise,
when p covers no such boundary point, we call it a pure singularity.

   The following definitions give the key topological relations under investigation.

Definition 2.9. (Contact ⊥). Two boundary points p ∈ ∂ϕ(M) and q ∈ ∂ϕ′ (M)
are in contact (denoted p⊥q ) if for all open neighbourhoods U and V of p and q
respectively,



                  U ⊓ V := ϕ−1 (U ∩ ϕ(M)) ∩ ϕ′−1 (V ∩ ϕ′ (M)) ̸= ∅.                  (2)

Definition 2.10. (Separate ∥). Two boundary points p ∈ ∂ϕ(M) and q ∈ ∂ϕ′ (M)
are separate (denoted p ∥ q ) if there exist open neighbourhoods U and V of p and q
respectively such that ϕ−1 (U ∩ ϕ(M)) ∩ ϕ′−1 (V ∩ ϕ′ (M)) = ∅ .

3 An Application of the Endpoint Theorem
The objective of this section is to establish control over the behaviour of geodesics in a
neighbourhood of a central, incomplete geodesic λ. Specifically, our objective is to show
that if the curvature along λ is bounded in a suitably constructed neighbourhood, then
no other geodesic can remain within that neighbourhood and share the same endpoint
as λ. In Proposition 3.2 this result will be formalised, and with use of the Endpoint
Theorem, a new envelopment (M, M̂′ , ψ ′ ) will be constructed such that λ has the
endpoint q ∈ ∂ (ψ ′ (M)), and no other geodesic ends at q . In the subsequent sections
this will enable us to greatly simplify the structure of singular boundary points.

In the study of the abstract boundary construction, the cover relation between
abstract boundary points has been well investigated, except for the topological rela-
tionship between a pure singularity and a pure point at infinity. In this section we
provide insight into the structure of pure singularities of maximally extended pseudo-
Riemannian manifolds. In the case where C is the class of geodesics with affine
parameter, and intertwined geodesics are not involved, it will be seen that a complete
analysis of this structure is possible.

Definition 4.8. A pure singularity set B is a boundary set such that q is a pure
singularity for all q ∈ B .

    A C l (0 ≤ l ≤ k ) pure singularity in an envelopment of a maximally extended
C pseudo-Riemannian manifold is locally C j inextendible, where j ≥ l. A C l pure
  k

singularity, by definition, will not be covered by any C l non-singular boundary set
containing at least one C l regular boundary point, nor does it cover any C l regular
boundary point, so it cannot be equivalent to any C l regular boundary point.

Proposition 4.9. Let (M, g, M̂, ψ, C ) be an envelopment of a maximally extended
pseudo-Riemannian manifold (M, g ). If the boundary point p ∈ ∂ψ (M) is only
approached by curves with bounded parameter, then p is a pure singularity.


Proof The fact that (M, g) is maximally extended and that the boundary point p is
approached by a curve with bounded parameter implies that p is an essential singularity. We
need to check if the essential singularity is a directional singularity which covers a pure point
at infinity, since from the definition of a directional singularity and Theorem 1.1, for a max-
imally extended pseudo-Riemannian manifold, p can only cover a pure point at infinity q.
This cannot be the case, however, since, by definition, q must be approached by a curve with
unbounded parameter, and p covers q, so it would be the case that the curve also approaches
p with unbounded parameter. This would contradict the assumption of the proposition. Thus
p is not a directional singularity, and so it is a pure singularity.                           □

4.2 An ordering relation and the existence or non-existence of
    minimal elements
In this section we will explain why it is not always necessary to consider a mini-
mal element under the cover relation, since Proposition 4.10 may provide a natural
termination point for the chain.
   Let (M, g, C, M̂, ψ ) be an envelopment of a maximally extended pseudo-
Riemannian manifold (M, g ) where C is the class of geodesics with affine parameter.
For the abstract boundary construction, boundary points are compared under the
equivalence relation using the mutual covering relation. To equip the abstract bound-
ary with a partial order, we will use the order on the set B (M) of all abstract boundary
points given by

                                  α ⪰ β ⇔ α covers β.

This is both transitive and reflexive and is a partial order on the set of all abstract
boundary points.

    We will now show lower bound candidature for a particular totally ordered subset
of the abstract boundary. Given an abstract boundary directional singularity σ0 = [p0 ]
where p0 ∈ ∂ψ (M) (an element of the a-boundary which has a directional singularity
representative p0 ), construct a cover relation chain for some index set A by

                                 C = {σα }α∈A ⊂ B(M)


                                            19
with
1. σ0 ∈ C
2. C is totally ordered by ⪰: for every α, β , α ≤ β ⇒ σα ⪰ σβ .
So we are considering a totally ordered chain C ⊂ B (M) (totally ordered by ⪰) which
contains a directional singularity σ0 ∈ C .

    A longstanding unresolved question related to the abstract boundary classifica-
tion, is whether or not a directional singularity must always cover a pure singularity
of another embedding. We note that since p0 ∈ ∂ψ (M) is a directional singularity, it
is approached by a geodesic γ with bounded affine parameter. We will require that
every element σα in the chain C is approached by γ . From the abstract boundary
classification scheme, this constrains every σα , for 0 < α, to be either a directional
singularity or a pure singularity.

    Suppose that in the chain C there exists a first α > 0 such that σα is a pure
singularity. This means that for all β > α, σβ is also a pure singularity. There is
therefore no need to consider a minimal element for this chain, since the existence
of σα answers the above question for the directional singularity σ0 , and so we can
truncate the chain at σα .

    It is certainly still possible, on the other hand, that C is an infinite chain consist-
ing entirely of directional singularities. We are aware that our ability to answer this
question for any given directional singularity relates to the existence, or otherwise,
of intertwined geodesics in the envelopments associated with each σα , α > 0. The
following corollary to Proposition 4.10 provides a resolution in the case where Propo-
sition 3.2 is used to produce an element σα in the chain for which no pair of geodesics
approaching σα is intertwined.


Corollary 4.12. Let (M, g, M̂, ψ, C ) be an envelopment of a maximally extended
pseudo-Riemannian manifold (M, g ), where C is the class of geodesics with affine
parameter, with p ∈ ∂ (ψ (M)) a directional singularity, and γ : [0, b) → M (where
b ∈ R+ ) is a non-self-intersecting geodesic in C without limit points in M. Suppose
ψ (γ ) → p. By Proposition 3.2, there exists another embedding ψ ′ such that ψ ′ (γ ) →
q ∈ ∂ (ψ ′ (M)), p ▷ q and no other geodesic in C ends at q . Suppose that no pair of
geodesics in C , each of which approaches q , is intertwined in (M, g, M̂′ , ψ ′ , C ). Then
γ (which has the endpoint q ) is the only geodesic which approaches q and q is a pure
singularity.


Proof The proof proceeds as for the proof of Proposition 4.10 up to the point where it is
established that γ is the only geodesic which approaches q. It approaches q as an endpoint
and, by assumption, has bounded parameter. It cannot be the case that q covers a pure
point at infinity of another embedding, as this would imply the existence of a geodesic with
unbounded parameter which approaches the pure point at infinity, and therefore also would


                                            20
approach q. Since γ is the only geodesic which approaches q, it follows that q is a pure
singularity.                                                                          □

    Note that for this corollary it is assumed that the geodesic γ has bounded parameter
and that ψ (γ ) → p. We recall that Proposition 3.2 still applies when the geodesic
γ has unbounded parameter. In this case, if the other conditions of Corollary 4.12
are satisfied, then q is a pure point at infinity. If q corresponds to the element σα of
the chain C , then this is where the chain should be truncated. Although this chain
does not provide information regarding the pure singularity part of the directional
singularity, it does separate out the pure point at infinity, which is also an important
part of unravelling directional singularities and the desired provision of an optimal
embedding for the pseudo-Riemannian manifold.

the Schwarzschild spacetime. We will show that the Penrose compactification of the
maximal extension of the Schwarzschild spacetime is an optimal embedding following
the definition provided in [12].

   (1) Partial cross section: since ψ is an envelopment, σψ = {[p] : p ∈ ∂ψ (M)} is a
partial cross section (Defn. 7.2.6 of [12]).

    (2) Ideal admissible partial cross section: the Kruskal-Szekeres maximal extension
of the Schwarzschild spacetime is at least C 2 inextendible. Therefore the set Br (M)
of all ideal regular abstract boundary points of (M, g ) is the empty set. It is evident
that for each [p] ∈ σψ , where p ∈ ∂ψ (M), there exists an open neighbourhood N of p
such that {[s] : s ∈ N ∩ ∂ψ (M)} ⊂ σψ . From Defn. 7.2.30 of [12], this implies that
σψ is an ideal admissible partial cross section.

    (3) Maximal ideal admissible partial cross section: suppose that σψ is not a maxi-
mal ideal admissible partial cross section (see Defn. 7.2.31 of [12]). Then there exists
an ideal admissible partial cross section σ which contains σψ as a proper subset,
which means that there exists an abstract boundary point [p] which is an element of
σ but which is not contained in σψ . There exists an envelopment ϕ : M → M̂ where
p ∈ ∂ϕ(M). Consider a sequence {xi } ⊂ M without limit points in M such that
ϕ(xi ) → p ∈ ∂ϕ(M). Because the Penrose maximal extension of the Schwarzschild
spacetime is compact, every infinite sequence in ψ (M) has a limit point in ψ (M).
Since the sequence {xi } ⊂ M has no limit points in M, it follows that there
exists an infinite subsequence {xij } ⊂ {xi } such that ψ (xij ) → q ∈ ∂ψ (M). Thus
ϕ(xij ) → p ∈ ∂ϕ(M) and ψ (xij ) → q ∈ ∂ψ (M) implying that the abstract boundary
points [p] and [q ] are not separate (Defn. 7.2.3 of [12]). Since σ is a partial cross
section, it must be the case then that [p] = [q ] which contradicts that [p] is not
contained in σψ . Thus σψ is a maximal ideal admissible partial cross section.

    From Defn. 7.3.1 of [12], since σψ is a maximal ideal admissible partial cross
section, the envelopment ψ for the Penrose compactification of the maximal extension
of the Schwarzschild spacetime is indeed an optimal embedding.

    It remains to consider how Wheeler’s Definitions 5.4, 5.5 and 5.6 apply to the
envelopment ψ . Due to the possible presence of directional singularities occurring
in other envelopments of the maximally extended Schwarzschild spacetime, we will
only consider these definitions with respect to pure singularity abstract boundary
points arising from the envelopment ψ . Furthermore, we need only consider the pure
singularities of this envelopment which lie to the future, i.e. with t′′ = π/2.

6 Discussion
The title of this paper “The relationship between spacetime singularities and regions
at infinity" is intimately connected with the following conjecture proposed by Wheeler
in [13].

Conjecture A. Under physically relevant choices of curve families C on a smooth
Lorentzian manifold (M, g ), (e.g. geodesics with affine parameter, C 1 curves with
generalised affine parameter, the causal subfamilies of either of these, etc.), points in
I (M) and Sp (M) are separated from each other.

In this conjecture I (M) is the collection of abstract boundary pure points at infinity
and Sp (M) is the collection of pure singularity abstract boundary points.

    For the class C of geodesics with affine parameter, for example, this conjecture
raises the immediate question of what sort of geodesics can approach a pure singu-
larity boundary point. From the definition, a pure singularity must be approached
by a geodesic with bounded parameter, but can it also be approached by a geodesic
with unbounded affine parameter? If this is the case, the pure singularity boundary
point could be in contact with a pure point at infinity of another envelopment, whilst
not covering it (which would make it a directional singularity), and so the two asso-
ciated abstract boundary points would not be separate. The analogous question for
pure points at infinity has a straightforward answer; pure points at infinity are only
approached by geodesics with unbounded affine parameter.

    In this paper we have progressed the understanding of these questions by focussing
on the class of affinely parametrised geodesics in maximally extended pseudo-
Riemannian manifolds. From Theorem 1.1 we know that these manifolds may have
an associated abstract boundary point which is a directional singularity and, if this
is the case, it must cover an abstract boundary point which is a pure point infinity.
By definition, a directional singularity must also be approached by a geodesic with


                                                       32
bounded parameter, and it remains an open question as to whether, in general, the
directional singularity must always cover a pure singularity of another envelopment. If
it does cover a pure singularity, are the pure point at infinity and the pure singularity
covered by the directional singularity necessarily separate?

    In Section 3 Proposition 3.2, which uses the Endpoint Theorem, establishes a very
general result for an n-dimensional, pseudo-Riemannian manifold (M, g ). If there
exists a geodesic γ which, under an envelopment of (M, g ), ends at a boundary point
p, then there always exists another envelopment in which the geodesic ends at the
boundary point q , no other geodesic ends at q , and the boundary point p covers the
boundary point q . Obviously this result has great utility in separating out different
components of boundary points, for example, when a geodesic with bounded param-
eter ends at a directional singularity.

    The concept of a pair of intertwined geodesics is considered in Section 4. First
introduced by Chruściel, the definition proposed by Graf and Beld-Serrano is given,
which is envelopment independent, followed by a new definition that we provide based
on the abstract boundary framework, which relates to a particular envelopment, and is
therefore envelopment dependent. It transpires that the non-existence of intertwined
geodesics is critical to our ability to answer the questions posed in this discussion.

    Using the new definition, for maximally extended pseudo-Riemannian manifolds,
when the situation detailed two paragraphs above is considered, for the case when p is
a pure singularity, Corollary 4.11 shows that if no pair of geodesics which approaches
q is intertwined, then the geodesic γ has bounded parameter, and no other geodesic
approaches q . This means that, from the pure singularity p, we have separated out
the pure singularity q which is only approached by geodesics with bounded param-
eter (indeed it is only approached by γ which has the endpoint q ). Furthermore, in
Corollary 4.12 we establish that if p is a directional singularity and the geodesic γ
which ends at p has bounded parameter, then q is a pure singularity and γ is the
only geodesic which approaches q . So in this case, from a directional singularity, we
have produced a pure singularity q which is only approached by one geodesic with
bounded parameter.

    In Section 4.2, when we considered a chain of abstract boundary points ordered
by the covering relation, we examined the case where one of the elements of the
chain is a directional singularity abstract boundary point [p], and p is the endpoint
of a geodesic γ with bounded parameter. Then if Proposition 3.2 can be employed
to form another envelopment, where γ ends at the boundary point q , which forms
the next element [q] of the chain, and no pair of intertwined geodesics approaches q ,
then [q ] is a pure singularity abstract boundary point which is only approached by
the geodesic γ . Also, if the geodesic γ has unbounded parameter, then [q ] is a pure
point at infinity abstract boundary point. In both cases, it is the production of an
envelopment without intertwined geodesics which enables us to truncate the covering
chain at either a pure singularity or pure point at infinity abstract boundary point.



                                           33
In Section 4.3 we present a definition for the maximal g-boundary in the abstract
boundary context, and show that for a maximally extended spacetime (M, g ), the
boundary of the future g-boundary extension is a pure singularity set.

   In Section 5 we analyse the definitions and results of the preceding sections with
respect to the Schwarzschild solution. In particular, we consider the envelopment
corresponding to the Penrose diagram for the maximally extended Schwarzschild
spacetime. We establish that there are no intertwined geodesic pairs inside the event
horizon, and thereby determine for the Penrose diagram, that all boundary points
(π/2, x′′ ) and (−π/2, x′′ ), where −π/2 < x′′ < π/2, are curvature singularities and
abstract boundary pure singularities which are only approached by geodesics with
bounded parameter. The remaining boundary points are all pure points at infinity.
This enables us to show that the Penrose diagram is an optimal embedding in the
abstract boundary context.

    Finally, we test Wheeler’s definitions of a black hole and an event horizon for
the Penrose diagram optimal embedding and find that they work well and produce
the expected result. We did, however, use a slightly modified definition of a singular
neighbourhood U , requiring only that every pure singularity abstract boundary point
arising from the Penrose diagram is strongly attached to U . It is not possible to have
complete knowledge of all envelopments of a spacetime, including if a directional
singularity exists in one of them, and whether it covers a pure singularity in another
envelopment.

    We believe that Wheeler’s definitions will be amenable to application and work
well when they are restricted to an optimal embedding for a solution. This brings
us back to Conjecture A which we don’t think will be true in general, as there may
exist envelopments with a boundary point which is a pure singularity approached
by a geodesic with unbounded affine parameter, raising the possibility that it is in
contact with a pure point at infinity of another envelopment (and so not separate).
In a future paper we will explore this possibility further, and will look for conditions
other than the non-existence of pairs of intertwined geodesics to separate out a pure
singularity from a directional singularity, and to obtain pure singularities which are
only approached by geodesics with bounded parameter.
